\begin{abstract}
The rapid proliferation of autonomous multi-turn agentic workflows has exacerbated the memory capacity bottleneck in Large Language Model (LLM) inference accelerators. In modern agentic sequences spanning $32\text{k}$ to $128\text{k}+$ tokens ($L=64, H_{\text{kv}}=8, d_k=128$, \texttt{float16}), Key-Value (KV) cache memory footprints expand to $17.18\text{ GB}$ ($16.00\text{ GiB}$) per active $64\text{k}$-token agent stream ($8.59\text{ GB}$ / $8.00\text{ GiB}$ at $32\text{k}$ tokens). Even under multi-chip tensor-parallel weight and KV-cache sharding or $32\text{k}\text{--}64\text{k}$ context distributions, this memory demand precipitates an acute capacity cliff on accelerators such as the Google Cloud TPU v6e Trillium (32~GB HBM per chip), exhausting non-weight HBM capacity after only 3 to 8 concurrent sequences. Existing virtualized inference engines (e.g., PagedAttention / vLLM and SGLang / RadixAttention) mitigate Out-Of-Memory exceptions via reactive demand paging to Host DRAM over PCIe/CXL. However, servicing a synchronous full-layer demand page fault ($64 \times 64\text{ KiB} = 4\text{ MiB}$ across $L=64$ layers, or $1.87\,\mu\text{s}$ of raw wire serialization per single-layer $64\text{ KiB}$ block at $35\text{ GB/s}$ effective PCIe Gen5 bandwidth plus descriptor ring wakeup and dirty writeback) incurs severe pipeline stalls ($0.12\text{ ms}$ to $0.50\text{ ms}$ per faulted step against $0.16\text{ ms}$ ideal compute), causing matrix execution units to idle for 40\% to 70\% of execution time. Furthermore, classical page eviction policies (LRU/LFU) are attention-blind: they inadvertently evict initial prompt attention sinks and active tool schemas while preserving ephemeral chain-of-thought scratchpad tokens.

To overcome these constraints, we present \textbf{Speculative Epistemic KV-Cache Paging (SE-Paging)} governed by the \textbf{Epistemic Least-Likely-Attended (ELLA)} eviction protocol. SE-Paging couples the agent's semantic execution graph and turn-smoothed epistemic branch entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ to physical memory management: (1) an Epistemic Working Set Estimator (EWSE) projects future attention mass $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$, permanently pinning attention sinks and retaining active/idle tool schemas and DAG-boosted history while decaying idle tool schemas below DAG-predicted history within $\approx 8$ steps ($\approx 1$ turn) and aggressively evicting resolved scratchpad pages; (2) an Asynchronous DMA Speculative Prefetcher (ADSP) with sealed-page write-through host mirroring overlaps Host-to-Device page transfers with active matrix computation across a $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}=2$-step horizon; and (3) ELLA establishes bounded competitive ratios relative to offline Belady MIN under both non-cascading trace-regular greedy eviction and randomized Predictive Marker phases. We evaluate SE-Paging in a downscaled $1:32$ trace-driven CPU-based physical tensor-buffer memory-hierarchy emulator (modeling a 2-layer $128\text{ KiB}$ slice per $B=16$-token page, $N_{\text{dev}}=96$ HBM pages [$12.00\text{ MiB}$], $N_{\text{host}}=2{,}500$ DRAM pages [$312.50\text{ MiB}$], and $976$ allocated physical pages [$16$ shared prefix + $48$ private pages/stream, or $15{,}616$ total tokens] across $K=20$ streams) parameterized to scaled TPU v6e Trillium and PCIe Gen5 timing, complemented by a 6-turn live trace replay on Google Cloud Vertex AI (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}) and parameterized multi-turn SWE-bench and Gorilla API emulation traces. Across $5{,}940$ logical page-lookup cycles ($11{,}880$ two-stage memory-subsystem operations per protocol) spanning $K \in \{4, 8, 12, 16, 20\}$ concurrent agent streams under $10.17\times$ physical ($13.33\times$ unshared logical) working-set overflow, SE-Paging elevates cache hit rate at $K=20$ from 0.20\% (LRU) and 1.21\% (LFU) to \textbf{28.23\%} ($28.31 \pm 0.38\%$ 5-seed mean), retains \textbf{98.64\%} empirical Query-Key (QK) RoPE dot-product attention mass, eliminates 46.8\% of memory pipeline stall overhead, and increases simulated device throughput by 49.3\% (+53.4\% in host wall-clock page-lookup throughput) over LRU.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{E}{nterprise} deployment of autonomous agent swarms built on foundation models (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Gemma 2, and Claude) represents a major workload shift in cloud computing. In frameworks such as the Google Agent Development Kit (ADK) and Vertex AI Agent Builder, agents engage in prolonged, multi-turn reasoning workflows involving hierarchical sub-goal decomposition, external tool invocations via OpenAPI schemas~\cite{patil2024gorilla}, and structured artifact generation~\cite{zheng2024sglang, gao2024cachedattention}.

\subsection{The KV-Cache HBM Capacity Cliff}
Unlike traditional stateless NLP classification or single-turn dialogue, agentic sequences accumulate extensive conversational histories, tool schemas, and chain-of-thought scratchpads, routinely expanding context lengths from $32\text{k}$ to $128\text{k}+$ tokens~\cite{kwon2023efficient, xiao2023efficient}.

During auto-regressive transformer decoding, Key and Value activation vectors for every past token must remain memory-resident to avoid redundant quadratic recomputation. The physical memory required scales linearly with context length $S$, transformer layers $L$, KV heads $H_{\text{kv}}$, and head dimension $d_k$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kv_footprint}
\text{Memory}_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times L \times H_{\text{kv}} \times d_k \times S \times \text{bytes\_per\_elem}
\end{equation}
For a representative enterprise model ($L=64$, $H_{\text{kv}}=8$, $d_k=128$, \texttt{float16} with $\text{bytes\_per\_elem}=2$), a sequence of $S = 64\text{k} = 65{,}536$ tokens requires $17{,}179{,}869{,}184\text{ bytes} \approx \mathbf{17.18\text{ GB}}$ decimal ($\mathbf{16.00\text{ GiB}}$ binary) of dedicated High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) (or $8{,}589{,}934{,}592\text{ bytes} \approx \mathbf{8.59\text{ GB}}$ decimal / $\mathbf{8.00\text{ GiB}}$ binary for $S = 32\text{k} = 32{,}768$ tokens).

On modern cloud accelerators such as the Google Cloud TPU v6e Trillium (configured with 32~GB HBM in 256-chip 2D torus pods, following Google's custom TPU architectures~\cite{jouppi2023tpu}) or GPU nodes~\cite{sheng2023flexgen}, this memory demand triggers an immediate \textit{capacity cliff}: even under multi-chip tensor-parallel model-weight and KV-cache sharding (where 64-layer weights are partitioned across chips, leaving $12\text{--}24\text{ GiB}$ of non-weight HBM per chip) or $32\text{k}\text{--}64\text{k}$ context distributions with shared prefixes, a chip exhausts its remaining HBM pool after serving only 3 to 8 concurrent agent sequences, capping cluster concurrency.

\subsection{The Demand Paging Pipeline Stall Dilemma}
To prevent fatal Out-Of-Memory (OOM) aborts, modern serving runtimes (e.g., vLLM / PagedAttention~\cite{kwon2023efficient} and SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglang}) partition the KV-cache into discrete non-contiguous physical pages ($B = 16$ tokens per block) and implement reactive \textit{demand swapping} to Host DRAM over PCIe Gen5 or CXL, while multi-turn offloading systems such as CachedAttention~\cite{gao2024cachedattention} perform coarse-grained inter-turn session pre-loading across conversation boundaries. Because attending densely to all historical blocks on every token step under $>10\times$ HBM overcommit would force continuous PCIe thrashing over completed reasoning steps, production long-context agentic runtimes increasingly pair virtualized paging with \textit{selective segment-routed} or \textit{router-guided working-set paging}, where each decoding step attends over persistent attention sinks ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$), the executing stream's active local window ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$), and the orchestrator-routed active working-set segments ($\mathcal{W}_{\text{active\_seg}} \subseteq \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$).

However, even under selective working-set routing, intra-turn demand swapping remains fundamentally reactive: when the accelerator's matrix execution units encounter an unmapped tool-schema or history page during token generation, the decoding pipeline stalls. Over a PCIe Gen5 $\times 16$ interconnect ($64\text{ GB/s}$ theoretical unidirectional bandwidth, $\approx 35\text{ GB/s}$ effective under multi-stream contention), transferring a single-layer $64\text{ KiB}$ page block ($B=16, H_{\text{kv}}=8, d_k=128$) requires $\approx 1.87\,\mu\text{s}$ of raw wire serialization ($3.74\,\mu\text{s}$ per fused 2-layer $128\text{ KiB}$ slice, or $119.7\,\mu\text{s}$ across all $L = 64$ transformer layers totaling $4\text{ MiB}$ per 16-token block); combined with PCIe DMA descriptor ring wakeup and synchronous dirty-page writeback, a full-model page fault stalls token generation for \textbf{$0.12\text{ ms}$ to $0.50\text{ ms}$} ($0.288\text{--}0.384\text{ ms}$ in our calibrated parameterization vs.\ $0.160\text{ ms}$ ideal 64-layer compute)~\cite{sheng2023flexgen, lee2024infinigen}. Under high sequence concurrency, these synchronous demand stalls accumulate, causing matrix execution units to spend \textbf{40\% to 70\% of wall-clock time stalled waiting on memory}.

\subsection{The Attention-Blind Eviction Failure}
Furthermore, classical operating system page eviction policies (Least Recently Used [LRU], Least Frequently Used [LFU], FIFO) and existing token-pruning or single-step rehearsal heuristics are mismatched with multi-turn agentic transformer physics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Destruction of Attention Sinks and Tool Schemas:} Transformers concentrate massive attention mass on the initial 1 to 4 sink blocks~\cite{xiao2023efficient} and active OpenAPI tool definitions~\cite{patil2024gorilla}. Under LRU, as an agent generates thousands of intermediate reasoning tokens, initial prompt tokens and tool schemas are aged out to Host RAM. While static window methods like StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2023efficient} drop middle tokens permanently, Heavy-Hitter Oracle (H2O)~\cite{zhang2023h2o} tracks cumulative attention scores, Quest~\cite{tang2024quest} estimates page criticality from query-key bounding boxes within the current layer without multi-step DMA prefetching, and InfiniGen~\cite{lee2024infinigen} performs single-step layer-adjacent rehearsal prefetching (using skewed query-key projections in layer $l-1$ to prefetch layer $l$), none of these approaches satisfy multi-step PCIe lookahead horizons ($\tau_{\text{lookahead}} = 2$ steps ahead) during abrupt multi-turn agentic tool-call DAG transitions where an entire dormant tool schema or prior history segment suddenly regains high query-key attention alignment. Similarly, unlike purely static agentic workflow prefetchers (KVFlow~\cite{pan2025kvflow}) or prefix-tree LRU schedulers (SGLang / RadixAttention~\cite{zheng2024sglang}) that ignore live generation uncertainty, SE-Paging couples workflow DAG lookahead with turn-smoothed epistemic Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$.
    \item \textbf{Scratchpad Memory Pollution:} Intermediate chain-of-thought scratchpad tokens are predominantly transient: once an agent concludes a reasoning sub-step, its scratchpad tokens carry rapidly decaying query-key weight unless backtracking occurs. Classical LRU retains newly written scratchpad pages in fast HBM, displacing reusable history and tool schemas.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
In this paper, we propose \textbf{Hardware-Aware Speculative KV-Cache Paging (SE-Paging)} governed by the \textbf{Epistemic Least-Likely-Attended (ELLA)} eviction protocol for router-guided two-tier working-set paging. Our key contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Working Set Estimator (EWSE):} A formal priority formulation that projects future page attention mass $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$ from agent semantic segment tags, stream-indexed tool-call and history DAG lookahead $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$, and conditionally re-normalized action-delimiter epistemic Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous DMA Speculative Prefetcher (ADSP):} A non-blocking prefetcher restricted to lookahead-predicted non-resident candidates with sealed-page write-through host mirroring that dispatches Host-to-Device transfers at step $t = n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}$, overlapping PCIe/CXL transfer latency with matrix compute while eliminating synchronous dirty-page writebacks.
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Performance Guarantees:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Theorem 1 (Queued Zero-Stall Pipeline Overlap):} Establishes the step-indexed multi-page lookahead condition $\tau_{\text{lookahead}} \ge \left\lceil \frac{M_{\text{pre}}(n) T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)}{(1-\rho_{\text{bus}})T_{\text{compute}}} \right\rceil$ under an unbacklogged or steady-state stable DMA queue under which device stall cycles are identically zero.
        \item \textbf{Theorem 2 (Attention Mass Preservation):} Proves that ELLA scratchpad eviction beyond the pinned active window ($\delta = 64$ tokens) of the executing stream analytically bounds attention mass loss by $\epsilon_{\text{attn}} \le C\delta^{1-\gamma}\!\left(\frac{1}{\delta} + \frac{1}{\gamma-1}\right) \le \frac{C_{\text{scratch}}}{(\gamma - 1)\delta^{\gamma - 1}}$ ($\le 0.0261$ at $\delta=64$ and $\le 0.0105$ at $\delta=256$ from the intermediate integral bound), complemented by non-circular empirical Query-Key (QK) RoPE softmax attention evaluation showing $98.64\%$ retained attention mass ($96.80\%$ top-$k$ QK page recall).
        \item \textbf{Theorem 3 (Bounded Competitive Ratio):} Uses the competitive caching with predictions framework~\cite{lykouris2021competitive} on acyclic agentic execution graphs to establish expected competitive ratios of $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{greedy}}] \le 1 + \mathcal{O}(\eta_{\text{tot}}/\text{OPT} + \mathcal{H}_{\max})$ under non-cascading trace-regular greedy eviction and $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{marker}}] \le 2 + \mathcal{O}(\min(\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\ell_1}]/\text{OPT}}, \log N_{\text{dev}}))$ when paired with randomized Predictive Marker phases relative to offline Belady MIN~\cite{belady1966study}.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Downscaled Physical Tensor-Buffer Emulator, Multi-Turn Benchmark Generalization \& Live Vertex AI Trace Replay:} We evaluate SE-Paging in a downscaled $1:32$ CPU-based physical tensor-buffer emulator (modeling a 2-layer $128\text{ KiB}$ slice per page, $N_{\text{dev}}=96$ HBM pages [$12.00\text{ MiB}$], $N_{\text{host}}=2{,}500$ DRAM pages [$312.50\text{ MiB}$], and $976$ allocated physical pages [$16$ shared prefix + $48$ private pages/stream, or $15{,}616$ total tokens] across $K=20$ streams) across $5{,}940$ logical page-lookup cycles ($11{,}880$ two-stage memory-subsystem operations per protocol), comparing against 11 baseline and ablation configurations (\texttt{DEMAND\_LRU}, \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU}, \texttt{SGLang/RadixAttention}~\cite{zheng2024sglang}, \texttt{StreamingLLM}~\cite{xiao2023efficient}, \texttt{H2O}~\cite{zhang2023h2o}, \texttt{Quest}~\cite{tang2024quest}, \texttt{CachedAttention}~\cite{gao2024cachedattention}, \texttt{InfiniGen}~\cite{lee2024infinigen}, \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} with and without write-through mirroring, and \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA}~\cite{pan2025kvflow}), plus a $\pm 50\%$ hyperparameter sensitivity study, a heterogeneous 20-prompt multi-tenant workload, parameterized multi-turn SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} and Gorilla OpenAPI~\cite{patil2024gorilla} (stateful BFCL-style multi-turn tool-switching) emulation traces, and a 6-turn live Google Cloud Vertex AI (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}) trace replay.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Virtualized KV-Cache and Offloading Systems:} PagedAttention / vLLM~\cite{kwon2023efficient} introduced OS-inspired block-level KV-cache virtualization ($B=16$ tokens) with reactive demand swapping, while SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglang} introduced RadixAttention for shared prefix-tree reuse with LRU leaf eviction. FlexGen~\cite{sheng2023flexgen} formulated static linear-programming offload schedules across GPU, CPU DRAM, and disk for offline batch throughput, and CachedAttention~\cite{gao2024cachedattention} overlapped inter-turn session restoration across multi-turn conversation boundaries. However, none of these systems prevent intra-turn page-fault stalls when an active agent dynamically switches between tool schemas and history blocks across sub-steps.
    \item \textbf{Token Pruning and Intra-Layer Sparse Attention:} StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2023efficient} retains initial attention sinks plus a static sliding window while permanently discarding middle tokens. H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o} evicts tokens with low cumulative attention scores, Quest~\cite{tang2024quest} selects top-$k$ pages per layer using query-key bounding boxes without asynchronous Host-to-Device DMA lookahead, and InfiniGen~\cite{lee2024infinigen} prefetches KV entries from layer $l-1$ to layer $l$ via skewed projections. While effective for single-step layer overlap, single-layer lookahead ($\tau=1$ layer) is insufficient to hide multi-page PCIe Gen5 transfer latency across multi-turn agentic state transitions.
    \item \textbf{Agent-Aware, Dynamic Workflow-Prediction, and Adaptive-TTL KV-Cache Management:} Recent agentic serving frameworks such as KVFlow~\cite{pan2025kvflow} and SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglang} exploit static agentic workflow graphs or radix trees to pre-warm or pin agent prefixes during structured tool-calling workloads~\cite{patil2024gorilla, jimenez2024swebench}. Emerging dynamic workflow-caching and offloading architectures extend this paradigm along three axes: (i) prediction-based multi-agent workflow schedulers (e.g., PBKV) that estimate inter-agent invocation probabilities across DAG nodes to pre-stage downstream agent contexts; (ii) cost-aware adaptive Time-To-Live (TTL) schedulers (e.g., Continuum) that estimate external tool-execution pause durations between turns to set job-level HBM retention TTLs; and (iii) two-tier segment offloading engines (e.g., LMCache and CachedAttention~\cite{gao2024cachedattention}) alongside region-aware exponential prompt decay and recallable host-memory page eviction (evaluated in our fixed-TTL, HBM-utilization-throttled adaptive-TTL, and observation-window ablations in Section~VI-C4). However, inter-agent invocation prediction and inter-turn pause TTLs operate at coarse macro-turn or cross-agent granularity: within a single agent's multi-step reasoning turn, when the model encounters high epistemic branching uncertainty during tool selection or error recovery, static DAG weights and turn-level TTL pins either retain resolved chain-of-thought scratchpad blocks unnecessarily, exhaust HBM when large tool catalogs (e.g., 32 tool-schema pages) are simultaneously pinned, or fail to trade off recent versus DAG-predicted history segments dynamically. SE-Paging bridges this gap by operating at intra-turn step granularity: it couples fine-grained semantic page classes ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$), asymptotic retention floors ($C_{\text{floor}}, C_{\text{hist}}$), continuous exponential idle-tool decay ($\lambda_{\text{tool}}$), and stream-indexed forward DAG distance $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$ with live action-delimiter epistemic Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ over grammar-valid schema tokens $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ and sealed-page write-through DMA mirroring.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture and Memory Hierarchy}

\subsection{Heterogeneous Memory Hierarchy Model}
We model an AI inference accelerator node (e.g., Google Cloud TPU v6e Trillium programmed via JAX/Pallas kernels~\cite{bradbury2018jax}) comprising two memory tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 0 (Device HBM):} High-bandwidth, capacity-constrained memory directly attached to Matrix Execution Units (MXUs) ($\text{BW}_{\text{HBM}} \ge 1.6\text{ TB/s}$, capacity $C_{\text{HBM}} \in \{32, 64\}\text{ GB}$).
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Host DRAM / CXL):} High-capacity system memory connected via PCIe Gen5 / CXL ($\text{BW}_{\text{bus}} \approx 32\text{--}64\text{ GB/s}$, capacity $C_{\text{Host}} \ge 512\text{ GB}$).
\end{itemize}

KV-cache is managed in fixed-size physical page blocks $P_k$ containing $B = 16$ tokens. For a single transformer layer ($H_{\text{kv}}=8, d_k=128$, \texttt{float16}), each page block occupies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:page_size}
\begin{aligned}
\text{Size}_{\text{1-layer}}(P_k) &= B \times 2 \times H_{\text{kv}} \times d_k \times 2\text{ bytes} \\
&= 65{,}536\text{ bytes } (64\text{ KiB})
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In our multi-layer block allocator (Section~VI-A), pairs of adjacent transformer layers are co-allocated into fused $128\text{ KiB}$ ($131{,}072\text{ byte}$) physical transfer units to amortize PCIe DMA descriptor ring overhead while permitting layer-pipelined DMA interleaving and sub-stack preemption across concurrent streams without blocking on a monolithic $4\text{ MiB}$ transfer. In a full 64-layer deployment, the 32 fused 2-layer slices of logical block $P_k$ share a single logical page-table descriptor and priority score $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$ equipped with a 32-bit layer-residency bitmap (\texttt{slice\_mask}); while uniform full-stack 32-slice block residency trades fine-grained layer-heterogeneous cache budgeting for $\mathcal{O}(1)$ page-table metadata overhead, if sub-stack preemption interrupts a 64-layer transfer after slice $s < 32$, layers $1\dots 2s$ execute while the remaining slices resume at the head of the DMA queue prior to layer $2s+1$, and partially resident blocks ($0 < \text{popcount}(\texttt{slice\_mask}) < 32$) remain pinned in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ until all 32 slices complete so partially populated blocks are never prematurely evicted. In our scaled $1:32$ slice emulator (modeling a representative 2-layer $128\text{ KiB}$ slice out of $L=64$ layers), per-slice raw DMA wire transfer latency $T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k) = 3.74\,\mu\text{s}$ ($4.56\,\mu\text{s}$ effective slice transfer latency under $\rho_{\text{bus}} = 0.18$ background bus contention), per-slice ideal matrix compute time $T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} = 5.00\,\mu\text{s}$, and scaled descriptor wakeup / writeback penalties ($T_{\text{wakeup}} = 4.44\,\mu\text{s}$, representing the $1:32$ emulator's amortized per-slice share of $142.08\,\mu\text{s}$ full-stack host page-fault handling and DMA descriptor ring initiation for a non-preempted 32-slice transfer—where sub-stack preemption after slice $s < 32$ would incur an additional hardware ring wakeup upon resumption—and $T_{\text{wb}} = 3.00\,\mu\text{s}$, yielding $4.44 + 4.56 = 9.00\,\mu\text{s}$ per clean slice fault or $32 \times (4.44 + 4.56) = 142.08 + 145.92 = 288.0\,\mu\text{s}$ across 64 layers, and $4.44 + 4.56 + 3.00 = 12.00\,\mu\text{s}$ per dirty slice fault or $384.0\,\mu\text{s}$ across 64 layers) scale uniformly by $1:32$ relative to a full 64-layer step ($T_{\text{DMA}}^{\text{64L}} \approx 119.7\,\mu\text{s}\text{ raw} / 145.92\,\mu\text{s}\text{ effective}, T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{64L}} = 160.0\,\mu\text{s}$), preserving exact dimensionless overlap ratios, page-replacement hit rates, and working-set overflow ratios.

\subsection{Categorical Page Classification and Boundary Resolution}
As tokens are ingested or generated by the orchestrator (e.g., Google ADK or SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglang}), lightweight structural delimiters (\texttt{<|system|>}, \texttt{<|tool\_schema|>}, \texttt{<|history|>}, \texttt{<|thought|>}) tag each token span in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time with a persistent underlying structural class ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$), while dynamically maintaining an active sliding window ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{System Anchor ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$):} System prompts and invariant instructions acting as persistent attention sinks~\cite{xiao2023efficient}.
    \item \textbf{Tool Schema ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}$):} OpenAPI function declarations and JSON schema contracts. Shared tool schemas ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{shared}}$) reside in the fixed-offset 16-page swarm prefix ($4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} + 12\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{shared}}$, positions $0\dots 255$) across all $K$ streams so pre-computed RoPE key embeddings remain position-invariant across streams (whereas private dynamically injected tool schemas $\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{priv}}$ occupy stream-local page descriptors).
    \item \textbf{Active Generation Window ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}$):} A temporary sliding-window protection applied to the 4 most recent $B=16$-token blocks ($W_{\text{local}} = 64$ tokens at block-aligned boundaries, comprising the 1 unsealed active tail block in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}}$ at $\Delta t_{\text{win}}=0$ plus the 3 most recently sealed blocks at $\Delta t_{\text{win}} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) of the currently executing stream $S_{i^*(t)}$.
    \item \textbf{Conversation History ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}$):} Verified multi-turn user queries and committed tool outputs.
    \item \textbf{Intermediate Scratchpad ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$):} Ephemeral chain-of-thought reasoning tokens from completed turns.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Dominant-Class Boundary and Non-Demoting Sliding-Window Rule:} The page allocator supports two boundary modes: (i) \textit{contiguous sub-block packing with dominant-class promotion} (used in our Section~VI benchmarks so every sealed page maintains full $B=16$-token occupancy and zero internal fragmentation), where any $B=16$-token block straddling two delimiter spans dynamically promotes its persistent class $c(P_k) \in \{\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}\}$ under the dominance hierarchy $\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \succ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \succ \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \succ \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$ upon filling 16 tokens; and (ii) \textit{strict delimiter sealing}, where structural delimiter transitions immediately seal the current block prior to reaching 16 tokens (exiting $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}}$ and triggering background write-through mirroring to Host DRAM) so ephemeral scratchpad tokens never share a physical frame with higher-tier segments. In both modes, all 4 blocks of the currently executing stream's active window $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$ (the 1 unsealed tail block at $\Delta t_{\text{win}} = 0$ plus the 3 most recently sealed blocks at $\Delta t_{\text{win}} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ in $W_{\text{local}}$) are structurally excluded from $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})$ during step $t$ (Invariant~1) and assigned active-window score $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i}(P_k, t) \triangleq 6 \times 10^8\exp(-\kappa \Delta t_{\text{win}})\mathbb{I}[P_k \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{active},i} \wedge i = i^*(t)]$ via $\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_i(P_k, t) = \max(\mathcal{A}_{\text{class}, i}(P_k, t), \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i}(P_k, t))$. Setting the active-window base weight to $6 \times 10^8 > 5 \times 10^8$ ensures that an anchor ($10^9$) inside $W_{\text{local}}$ is never demoted, while sealed $W_{\text{local}}$ blocks of the executing stream $S_{i^*(t)}$ strictly outrank peer streams' active tool schemas ($5 \times 10^8$) and revert to $\mathcal{A}_{\text{class}, i}(P_k, t)$ when $S_{i^*(t)}$ yields or advances past 4 blocks. This prevents $K \times 3 = 60$ dormant sealed window blocks across $K=20$ streams from monopolizing the 96-page HBM arena; if a yielded stream $S_i$'s sealed local-window scratchpad block is evicted while dormant, ADSP automatically includes any non-resident block of $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i}$ in $S_i$'s lookahead prefetch batch at step $t = n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}$ (or, if capacity-gated, services it as a synchronous pre-step demand fault accounted directly in $N_{\text{miss}}^{\text{clean}}$ at $9.00\,\mu\text{s}$/slice prior to local-window attention).

\section{The Speculative Epistemic Paging Protocol}

\subsection{Epistemic Attention Scoring Formulation}
\begin{definition}[Projected Attention Score]
Let $t \in \mathbb{N}$ denote the global execution / page-lookup step index scheduled round-robin across the $K$ active streams, and let $t_i = \lfloor t/K \rfloor$ denote the stream-local step index of stream $S_i$ (where each macroscopic agentic reasoning turn spans $8\text{--}12$ stream-local execution steps). For a KV-cache page $P_k$ evaluated for agent stream $S_i$ at step $t$, let $\Delta t_i(P_k, t) = t_i - t_{\text{last\_access},i}(P_k) \ge 0$ denote the elapsed stream-local step count since the semantic segment containing $P_k$ was last generated, selected by the router, or accessed by stream $S_i$ (where $t_{\text{last\_access},i}(P_k)$ records the stream-local step index of $S_i$'s most recent reference; equivalently, when indexed by global step $t$, the per-step decay rates $\lambda, \lambda_{\text{tool}}, \mu$ are scaled by $1/K$ so per-turn decay is invariant across concurrency levels $K$), and let $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1} \cup \{\infty\}$ denote the shortest-path positive stream-local forward step distance along stream $S_i$'s mode-selected workflow branch ($\arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$, or minimum distance across the top-$B_{\text{top}}=2$ branches when $\mathcal{H}_i(t) > 0.50$) from its current node to the next future node referencing $P_k$'s segment. Concretely, the orchestrator maintains a lightweight \textit{segment-adjacency lookahead table} indexed by candidate action token $v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ that maps each branch decision $v$ to: (a) its immediate ($\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}^{(v)}(P_k, t) = 1$) OpenAPI tool-schema pages $P_k \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}$, (b) downstream ($\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}^{(v)}(P_k, t) \in \{2, 3, \dots\}$) tool-transition schemas in the declared ADK workflow graph, and (c) prior-turn conversation history segments $P_k \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}$ linked by data-dependency edges (e.g., the prior tool-output or user-specification segment consumed by action $v$, or the parent turn segment targeted by a verification/backtrack delimiter). Its per-stream class score $\mathcal{A}_{\text{class},i}(P_k, t)$ under ELLA is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ella_score}
\begin{aligned}
&\mathcal{A}_{\text{class},i}(P_k, t) \triangleq \\
&\left\{\begin{aligned}
&10^9, \hfill \text{if } c(P_k) = \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \\[3pt]
&5\!\times\!10^8\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}(P_k, t) + (1\!-\!\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}(P_k, t)) \\
&\; \times\!\Bigl(W_{\text{tool}} e^{-\lambda_{\text{tool}}\Delta t_i(P_k, t)} + C_{\text{floor}} + \eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}\Bigr), \\
&\hfill \text{if } c(P_k) = \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \\[4pt]
&\max\!\Bigl(C_{\text{hist}},\, W_{\text{hist}} e^{-\lambda \Delta t_i(P_k, t)}(1 - \mathcal{H}_i(t) + \epsilon)\Bigr) \\
&\; + B_{\text{DAG}}\mathbb{I}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) + \eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}, \\
&\hfill \text{if } c(P_k) = \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \\[4pt]
&W_{\text{scratch}} \exp(-\mu \Delta t_i(P_k, t))\,\mathcal{H}_i(t) + \eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}, \\
&\hfill \text{if } c(P_k) = \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}
\end{aligned}\right.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with effective per-stream score $\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_i(P_k, t) = \max(\mathcal{A}_{\text{class},i}(P_k, t), \mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i}(P_k, t))$ using $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i}(P_k, t)$ from Section~III-B, and global shared-page priority across all referencing streams $\mathcal{O}(P_k)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:global_score}
\mathcal{A}(P_k, t) \triangleq \max_{S_i \in \mathcal{O}(P_k)} \tilde{\mathcal{A}}_i(P_k, t)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}(P_k, t) = \mathbb{I}[c(P_k)=\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \wedge \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) \le d_{\text{look}}] \in \{0, 1\}$ and $\mathbb{I}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) = \mathbb{I}[c(P_k)=\mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \wedge \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) \le d_{\text{look}}] \in \{0, 1\}$ equal $1$ when the orchestrator's workflow DAG or grammar-constrained router predicts a reference to tool schema or history page $P_k$ within stream-local lookahead depth $d_{\text{look}} = 2$ steps (and $0$ otherwise); $W_{\text{tool}} = 500$, $W_{\text{hist}} = 600$, and $W_{\text{scratch}} = 100$ are the initial class weights; $C_{\text{floor}} = 200$ and $C_{\text{hist}} = 120$ are the asymptotic tool-schema and verified-history retention floors; $B_{\text{DAG}} = 350$ is the history lookahead boost; $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1} \in [0, 1]$ is the inverse forward step distance ($0$ if $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) = \infty$, scaled by $\eta_{\text{DAG}} = 10$ across all non-anchor tiers so an idle tool schema at $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i} = 3$ steps ahead outranks permanently dormant tools); and stream-local step decay hyperparameters are calibrated on held-out multi-turn traces to $\kappa = 0.05\text{ block}^{-1}$ (over window block offset $\Delta t_{\text{win}} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$), $\lambda = 0.08\text{ step}^{-1}$, $\lambda_{\text{tool}} = 0.08\text{ step}^{-1}$, $\mu = 0.45\text{ step}^{-1}$, and $\epsilon = 0.10$ ($\mu \gg \lambda = \lambda_{\text{tool}} > \kappa > 0$). For shared pages ($|\mathcal{O}(P_k)| > 1$), let $S_{i^\dagger(P_k, t)} \in \arg\max_{S_i \in \mathcal{O}(P_k)} \tilde{\mathcal{A}}_i(P_k, t)$ denote the maximizing owner stream in Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_score}; ties in $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$ (and any internal co-maximizer ties for $i^\dagger$) are broken deterministically by $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i^\dagger}(P_k, t)^{-1}$ (evicting smaller $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i^\dagger}^{-1}$ first during victim selection and admitting larger $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i^\dagger}^{-1}$ first during prefetching), then maximizing owner stream index $i^\dagger$, oldest stream-specific LRU timestamp $t_{\text{last\_access},i^\dagger}(P_k)$, and finally lowest physical page index $k$.
\end{definition}

\textbf{Idle Tool-Schema Decay Horizon vs.\ DAG-Boosted History Preemption:} Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:ella_score}, a newly used tool schema entering the idle state ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}(P_k, t) = 0$ at $\Delta t_i = 0, \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i} = \infty$) has score $W_{\text{tool}} + C_{\text{floor}} = 500 + 200 = 700$, protecting it against immediate eviction during the current turn. Once dormant for $\Delta t_i > \ln(500/270)/0.08 \approx 7.70\text{ stream-local steps}$ ($\approx 1$ turn at $8\text{--}12$ stream-local steps/turn), its priority drops below $470$ (reaching $300.9$ by $\Delta t_i = 20$ stream-local steps), enabling a floored DAG-predicted history page ($\ge C_{\text{hist}} + B_{\text{DAG}} = 120 + 350 = 470$, or $480$ at $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i} = 1$) to preempt dormant tool schemas when resuming an earlier conversation branch. Meanwhile, because $t_{\text{last\_access},i}(P_k)$ and the segment-adjacency lookahead table for $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)$ are maintained at segment-to-page table granularity by the orchestrator (requiring at most $|\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| = 64$ table lookups at action-delimiter steps), descriptor state updates require $\mathcal{O}(1)$ time per page access (paired with an $\mathcal{O}(N_{\text{dev}})$ priority scan over the $N_{\text{dev}}=96$ HBM descriptors upon victim selection), avoiding any $\mathcal{O}(S)$ token-level scan or quadratic attention matrix materialization inside fused Pallas FlashAttention-2 kernels~\cite{dao2024flashattention2}.

\textbf{Action-Delimiter Epistemic Entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ and Multi-Branch Lookahead Coupling:} To prevent deterministic JSON punctuation and syntax tokens from driving entropy artificially toward zero, the orchestrator evaluates Shannon entropy exclusively when step $t$ coincides with a structural branch or action delimiter $m(t) \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{branch}}$ (e.g., \texttt{<|thought|>}, \texttt{<|tool\_call|>}, and tool-name selection tokens) over the grammar-valid candidate action-token subset $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ ($|\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| = 64$, or the prompt's fixed active schema set $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}^{\text{API}}$ intersected with returned $\text{Top-}K_{\text{log}}$ logprobs on commercial APIs), holding $\mathcal{H}_i(t) = \mathcal{H}_i(t-1)$ constant across non-delimiter decoding steps (while physical block sealing occurs every $B=16$ generated tokens or upon a delimiter transition). Raw token probabilities are conditionally re-normalized over $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ with Laplace smoothing floor $\epsilon_{\text{prob}} = 10^{-4}$ (applied when any candidate in $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ falls outside truncated API top-$K_{\text{log}}$ logprobs, and reducing to $\epsilon_{\text{prob}}=0$ when full logits over $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}$ are available) via $\tilde{p}_{i,m}(v) = \max(p_{i,m}(v), \epsilon_{\text{prob}}) / \sum_{u \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \max(p_{i,m}(u), \epsilon_{\text{prob}})$ so that $\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v) = 1$, and smoothed from neutral prior $\mathcal{H}_i(0) = 0.50$ via an exponential moving average with factor $\beta = 0.30$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:branch_entropy}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{H}_i(t) &= (1 - \beta)\mathcal{H}_i(t-1) \\
&\quad + \beta\!\left(-\frac{1}{\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)\ln \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)\right) \in [0, 1]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $0\ln 0 \triangleq 0$. While strict tier-bound separation (maximum unwindowed scratchpad score $W_{\text{scratch}} + \eta_{\text{DAG}} = 110 < \text{asymptotic floors } C_{\text{hist}} = 120 < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$) guarantees that neither dormant tool schemas nor verified history ever decay below unwindowed scratchpad pages as $\Delta t_i \to \infty$, modulating $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ drives three synergistic mechanisms: (1) \textit{intra-tier history crossover}, where high branch uncertainty ($\mathcal{H}_i(t) \to 1$) rapidly anneals unpredicted recent history pages $W_{\text{hist}}e^{-\lambda \Delta t_i}(1 - \mathcal{H}_i(t) + \epsilon)$ toward $C_{\text{hist}} = 120$, allowing older DAG-boosted history pages ($\ge 470$) to outrank recent turns during exploratory backtracking; (2) \textit{cross-stream scratchpad arbitration}, where across concurrent streams $S_i \ne S_j$, stream $S_i$ in high-uncertainty exploration ($\mathcal{H}_i(t) \to 1$) retains higher scratchpad priority than stream $S_j$ whose branching has resolved ($\mathcal{H}_j(t) \to 0$ via geometric EMA decay $(1-\beta)=0.7$ per delimiter, where scratchpad priority decays toward $\le 10$, i.e., $\eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$); and (3) \textit{entropy-gated runner-up branch protection and speculative admission}, where when $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \le 0.50$, $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)$ evaluates the single top-1 mode-selected branch $v_1 = \arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$ to focus PCIe bandwidth, whereas under high branching ambiguity ($\mathcal{H}_i(t) > 0.50$) the orchestrator selects the top-$B_{\text{top}}=2$ candidate branches $\{v_1, v_2\} = \text{Top-}2(\tilde{p}_{i,m})$ by calibrated action-token probability $\tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$ and evaluates $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) = \min_{v \in \{v_1, v_2\}} \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}^{(v)}(P_k, t)$ (breaking intra-tier candidate ties by expected inverse distance $\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}^{(v)}(P_k, t)^{-1}$). Because $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) \le d_{\text{look}} = 2$ sets $\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}(P_k, t) = 1$ or $\mathbb{I}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) = 1$ for pages belonging to either $v_1$ or $v_2$, this simultaneously protects already-resident runner-up branch pages from premature HBM eviction and admits non-resident runner-up branch pages into ADSP's $M_{\text{pre}}(n) \le M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 4$ speculative DMA prefetch batch (accounting for the $12.4 \pm 1.6$ unreferenced speculative prefetches on non-taken runner-up branches in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_k20} Footnote~$\ddagger$).

\subsection{Epistemic Least-Likely-Attended (ELLA) Eviction Invariant}
\begin{invariant}[ELLA Evictable Victim Selection]
Let $\tau_{\text{lookahead}} = 2$ steps denote the speculative lookahead horizon and let $M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 4$ pages/step denote the global batch prefetch cap (Section~V-A). Let $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM}) \triangleq \textit{Resident}_{\text{clean}}(\textit{HBM}) \setminus (\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)})$ denote the subset of HBM-resident sealed pages with completed Host DRAM write-through mirrors excluding system anchor pages ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$, score $10^9$), in-flight/admitted prefetch-pinned pages ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$, bounded by $\le \tau_{\text{lookahead}} M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 8$ pages globally), currently unsealed active tail pages across all streams ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}}$, $\le K$ pages), and all 4 active-window blocks of the currently executing stream ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$, adding at most $3$ sealed local-window blocks for $S_{i^*(t)}$). When allocating or prefetching a page requires a free frame in Device HBM, the controller evicts:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:victim_select}
P_{\text{victim}} = \arg\min_{P_j \in \textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})} \mathcal{A}(P_j, t)
\end{equation}
If $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM}) = \emptyset$ under extreme concurrency, ADSP suppresses speculative prefetch admission and, upon a mandatory synchronous demand fault, applies a two-stage fallback precedence: (i) first awaits completion of any in-flight write-through DMA descriptor on the lowest-scoring sealed page in $\textit{Resident}(\textit{HBM}) \setminus (\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)})$; and (ii) if no such unpinned sealed page exists, releases the lowest-scoring page in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ prior to ever evicting any $\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$ ($10^9$) or $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$ page. Under the single-page sub-cycle admission precondition $|\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| + K + 3 + \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} < N_{\text{dev}}$ (here $4 + 20 + 3 + 8 = 35 \ll 96$, or $|\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| + K + 3 + \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} + m_{\text{seg}} \le N_{\text{dev}}$ for simultaneous co-residency of an $m_{\text{seg}}$-page active working set), at least $N_{\text{dev}} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| - K - 3 - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 61$ evictable frames exist in HBM during normal operation prior to fallback (expanding to $N_{\text{dev}} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| - K - 3 = 69$ frames upon releasing $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ in Fallback Stage~ii), guaranteeing deadlock freedom.
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Asynchronous DMA Speculative Prefetcher and Write-Through Mirroring}
To avoid blocking Device-to-Host writebacks during eviction, SE-Paging implements \textbf{Asynchronous Sealed-Page Write-Through Mirroring}: while an active tail page in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active\_unsealed}}$ ($B=16$ tokens) is populated token-by-token across 16 decoding steps, it remains pinned in HBM; once all 16 token slots are filled (or a structural delimiter transition occurs) and the page is sealed, a single asynchronous background DMA copy mirrors the full $128\text{ KiB}$ ($4\text{ MiB}$ full-stack) clean page to its pre-allocated Host DRAM slot, avoiding $16\times$ partial-page PCIe write amplification (under strict delimiter sealing mode~ii, early-sealed frames transfer the full $128\text{ KiB}$ block with unpopulated trailing token slots zero-padded so DMA descriptor alignment remains uniform; because structural delimiters occur at macro-segment boundaries spaced $\ge 4$ tokens apart with mean effective occupancy $\bar{B}_{\text{eff}} \ge 12$ tokens/block, background write-through traffic remains well within $\rho_{\text{bus}} \le 0.18$). Because KV-cache tokens are append-only in causal transformer decoding, sealed pages are never mutated in-place; $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})$ selects clean mirrored frames (\texttt{is\_mirrored\_to\_host == True}) and only awaits an in-flight write-through descriptor if all mirrored frames are exhausted. To bridge stream-local DAG lookahead ($\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t) \le d_{\text{look}} = 2$ stream-local steps in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ella_score}, which continuously protects lookahead-predicted pages across all $K$ streams during HBM victim selection via Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_score}) with global round-robin DMA scheduling, at current dispatch step $t = n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}$ ($\tau_{\text{lookahead}} = 2$ global steps prior to target execution step $n = t + 2$), ADSP dispatches up to $M_{\text{pre}}(n) \le M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 4$ non-resident candidate pages specifically for target stream $S_{i^*(n)}$ where $i^*(n) = (i^*(t)+2)\bmod K$ scheduled at step $n = t + 2$. Crucially, while $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i}(P_k, t)$ in Section~III-B protects the currently executing stream $i^*(t)$, ADSP evaluates candidate and resident active-window priority for target stream $S_{i^*(n)}$ across the lookahead window $[n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}, n]$ using target-step active-window membership $\mathcal{A}_{\text{active}, i^*(n)}(P_k, n) = 6 \times 10^8\exp(-\kappa \Delta t_{\text{win}})\mathbb{I}[P_k \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(n)}]$ alongside its most recently updated epistemic state $\mathcal{H}_{i^*(n)}(t)$ and router predictions $\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i^*(n)}(P_k, t) = 1$ ($5 \times 10^8$) or $\mathbb{I}_{\text{DAG},i^*(n)}(P_k, t) = 1$ ($\ge 470$). Meanwhile, the batch of $\le 4$ pages already dispatched at step $t - 1$ for stream $S_{(i^*(t)+1)\bmod K}$ (scheduled at step $t + 1$) remains pinned in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ through step $t+1$. Every prefetch pin in $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ for batch $\mathcal{W}_{\text{spec}}(n)$ expires automatically at the conclusion of target global step $n$ (returning any unreferenced runner-up branch page to $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})$), so deferring DMA candidate dispatch until $t = n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}$ strictly maintains the global invariant $|\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}| \le \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 2 \times 4 = 8$ pages and prevents prefetched frames from prematurely displacing active frames needed by the intermediate $K-2$ streams scheduled during $[n-K+1, n-\tau_{\text{lookahead}})$. ADSP sorts non-resident candidates $P_{\text{cand}, r}$ for target step $n$ in descending order of target-step priority $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{cand}, r}, n)$ (breaking ties via Definition~1) against evictable HBM victims $P_{\text{victim}, r} \in \textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})$ in ascending order of $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t)$, and dispatches up to $M_{\text{pre}}(n) \le 4$ asynchronous Host-to-Device DMA transfers subject to the primary \textbf{cold-headroom admission condition} $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{cand}, r}, n) > \mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t)$ and $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$. Requiring $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$ for speculative DMA admission complements reactive demand paging in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ella_score}: whereas a mandatory synchronous demand fault on a DAG-boosted history page ($\ge 470$) is permitted to evict an idle tool schema that has decayed below $470$ after $\approx 8$ stream-local steps, speculative background prefetches only displace cold scratchpad frames ($\le 110$) and decayed unpredicted history pages near the floor ($[120, 200)$) without speculatively churning resident tool schemas ($\ge C_{\text{floor}} = 200$; for workloads with massive tool catalogs where dormant tool schemas saturate HBM in $[200, 250)$, an elevated threshold $C_{\text{floor}} + \epsilon_{\text{idle}} = 250$ or relative priority-ratio gate $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{cand}, r}, n)/\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) > 10^5$ is analyzed in Section~VI-C1).

\section{Formal Theoretical Analysis}

\subsection{Theorem 1: Queued Zero-Stall Pipeline Overlap Invariant}
\begin{theorem}[Zero-Stall Overlap Under Queued DMA]
Let $T_{\text{compute}}$ be the per-step execution latency of matrix execution units, let $T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k) = \text{Size}(P_k)/\text{BW}_{\text{bus}}$ be the raw Host-to-Device wire transfer time of page $P_k$ under persistent zero-wakeup DMA descriptor rings, let $\mathcal{W}_{\text{spec}}(n)$ denote the batch of non-resident pages admitted by ADSP at dispatch time $t_{\text{dispatch}} \le t_n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}} T_{\text{compute}}$ for target step $n$ with batch size $M_{\text{pre}}(n) = |\mathcal{W}_{\text{spec}}(n)| \le M_{\text{pre}}^{\max}$, let $\rho_{\text{bus}} \in [0, 1)$ denote non-prefetch background interconnect utilization (absorbing asynchronous write-through mirroring and host telemetry under the strict steady-state queue stability condition $\mathbb{E}[M_{\text{pre}}(n)]T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)/(1-\rho_{\text{bus}}) < T_{\text{compute}}$), and let $W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) \ge 0$ denote any residual prior-step DMA queue backlog at $t_{\text{dispatch}}$ (including any unfinished descriptors dispatched at step $n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}} - 1$ for step $n-1$). Whenever the queue is unbacklogged at dispatch ($W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) = 0$), intermediate steps $j \in \{n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}, \dots, n-1\}$ experience no unpredicted synchronous demand faults that preempt the FIFO ring (if an intermediate step $j$ references a page already queued in $\mathcal{W}_{\text{spec}}(n)$, the DMA controller coalesces the request in-place without duplicate transfer; step $j$ itself awaits descriptor completion and incurs $\Delta_{\text{stall}}(j) > 0$, after which the page is already resident by step $n$), and the speculative lookahead horizon satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lookahead_cond}
\tau_{\text{lookahead}} \ge \left\lceil \frac{M_{\text{pre}}(n) \cdot T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)}{(1 - \rho_{\text{bus}}) \cdot T_{\text{compute}}} \right\rceil
\end{equation}
(or more generally $\tau_{\text{lookahead}} \ge \lceil (W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) + \frac{M_{\text{pre}}(n) T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)}{1 - \rho_{\text{bus}}}) / T_{\text{compute}} \rceil$ when $W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) > 0$), and $M_{\text{pre}}(n)$ clean sealed victim frames in $\textit{Resident}_{\text{evictable}}(\textit{HBM})$ (disjoint from active working sets and in-flight pins $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ over $[t_{\text{dispatch}}, t_n)$, guaranteed by $|\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| + K + 3 + \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 35 \le N_{\text{dev}} = 96$) are available in HBM, target step $n$ experiences identically zero memory stall cycles ($\Delta_{\text{stall}}(n) \equiv 0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Across the $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}$ intermediate global steps $j \in \{n - \tau_{\text{lookahead}}, \dots, n-1\}$, elapsed wall-clock time satisfies $t_n - t_{\text{dispatch}} = \sum_{j=n-\tau_{\text{lookahead}}}^{n-1} (T_{\text{compute}} + \Delta_{\text{stall}}(j)) \ge \tau_{\text{lookahead}} \cdot T_{\text{compute}}$. Under FIFO non-preemptive servicing of admitted speculative descriptors across $[t_{\text{dispatch}}, t_n)$, residual queue backlog $W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) \ge 0$, net bus bandwidth $(1 - \rho_{\text{bus}})\text{BW}_{\text{bus}}$ available to ADSP prefetches, and write-through clean victim reclamation, the serialized completion time of the $r$-th page ($r \in \{1, \dots, M_{\text{pre}}(n)\}$) is $t_{\text{completion}}(P_{\text{cand}, r}) = t_{\text{dispatch}} + W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) + r \cdot T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)/(1 - \rho_{\text{bus}})$, and the completion time of all $M_{\text{pre}}(n)$ pages is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dma_completion}
t_{\text{completion}} = t_{\text{dispatch}} + W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) + \frac{M_{\text{pre}}(n) \cdot T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)}{1 - \rho_{\text{bus}}}
\end{equation}
When $W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) = 0$, substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:lookahead_cond} yields $t_n - t_{\text{dispatch}} \ge \tau_{\text{lookahead}} \cdot T_{\text{compute}} \ge M_{\text{pre}}(n) T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k) / (1 - \rho_{\text{bus}})$, which implies $t_{\text{completion}} \le t_n$. Because $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ protects all $\le \tau_{\text{lookahead}}M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 8$ admitted frames across steps $n-1$ and $n$ until their respective target steps conclude, all $M_{\text{pre}}(n)$ pages remain mapped in HBM at $t_n$, and $\Delta_{\text{stall}}(n) = \max(0, t_{\text{completion}} - t_n) = 0$. Concretely, under our Section~III-A parameterization ($T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)/(1-\rho_{\text{bus}}) = 3.74/0.82 = 4.56\,\mu\text{s}$, $T_{\text{compute}} = 5.00\,\mu\text{s}$, and $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}T_{\text{compute}} = 10.00\,\mu\text{s}$), an unbacklogged dispatch ($W_q = 0$) achieves $\Delta_{\text{stall}}(n) = 0$ for batch sizes $M_{\text{pre}}(n) \in \{1, 2\}$ ($4.56\,\mu\text{s}$ and $9.12\,\mu\text{s} \le 10.00\,\mu\text{s}$); across consecutive dispatches, $M_{\text{pre}}(n-1) \le 1$ drains completely within a single $5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ step ($W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) = 0$), whereas $M_{\text{pre}}(n-1) = 2$ leaves $W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) = 9.12 - 5.00 = 4.12\,\mu\text{s}$ (so an immediately following $M_{\text{pre}}(n) = 2$ batch completes at $4.12 + 9.12 = 13.24\,\mu\text{s} > 10.00\,\mu\text{s}$, leaving $M_{\text{part}}(n) = 1$ tail page in flight), and any burst of $M_{\text{pre}}(n) \in \{3, 4\}$ pages requires $13.68\text{--}18.24\,\mu\text{s} > 10.00\,\mu\text{s}$ and always produces $M_{\text{part}}(n) \in \{1, 2\}$ partially overlapped pages (directly explaining why $M_{\text{part}} > 0$ across all $K$ in Tables~\ref{tab:paging_telemetry} and~\ref{tab:ablation_k20}). Whenever $t_{\text{completion}} > t_n$, let $M_{\text{part}}(n) \triangleq |\{P_{\text{cand}, r} \in \mathcal{W}_{\text{spec}}(n) : t_{\text{completion}}(P_{\text{cand}, r}) > t_n\}|$ denote the count of admitted speculative tail pages whose DMA transfer remains in-flight at $t_n$. Step $n$ incurs the affine batch-level residual stall $\Delta_{\text{stall}}(n) \le \max\!\left(0,\, W_q(t_{\text{dispatch}}) + \frac{M_{\text{pre}}(n)T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k)}{1-\rho_{\text{bus}}} - \tau_{\text{lookahead}} T_{\text{compute}}\right)$, which is approximated in our slice emulator's accounting formula (Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_stall_accounting}) by the calibrated mean-value linear proxy $M_{\text{part}}(n) \times T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{part}}$ ($T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{part}} = 2.50\,\mu\text{s}$/page across $M_{\text{part}} = \sum_n M_{\text{part}}(n)$, verified within $0.42$ percentage points against an exact event-driven FIFO DMA queue simulation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dma_completion} in Section~VI-A).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem 2: Attention Mass Bounded Approximation}
\begin{theorem}[Attention Mass Preservation]
Under Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE~\cite{su2024roformer}) where unnormalized query-key exponentials on completed scratchpad tokens at relative token distance $|m - j| \ge \delta$ satisfy the trace-calibrated power-law envelope premise $\exp(q_m^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k}) \le C_{\text{num}} |m - j|^{-\gamma}$ ($\gamma > 1$) and pinned sink plus local-window tokens provide a strictly positive partition lower bound $Z_{\text{pinned}} > 0$ so that full-context normalized attention weights ($\sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_{m,j} = 1$) satisfy $\alpha_{m,j} \le C |m - j|^{-\gamma}$ with $C \triangleq C_{\text{num}} / Z_{\text{pinned}}$, conditioned on the executing stream's 4 active-window blocks and router-predicted $\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}$ working-set segments being resident in HBM, evicting scratchpad pages $\mathcal{P}_{\text{evicted}, i^*(t)}^{\text{scratch}}$ outside the executing stream's pinned active window at token distance $\ge \delta$ (where $\delta(r) = 3B + r = 48 + r \in [49, 64]$ for intra-block tail occupancy $r \in \{1, \dots, 16\}$, and $\delta = W_{\text{local}} = 64$ at block-aligned boundaries $r = 16$) under ELLA preserves at least $1 - \epsilon_{\text{attn}}$ of the full-context attention mass, where:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:attn_bound}
\epsilon_{\text{attn}} \le C\delta^{1-\gamma}\!\left(\frac{1}{\delta} + \frac{1}{\gamma - 1}\right) \le \frac{C_{\text{scratch}}}{(\gamma - 1)\delta^{\gamma - 1}}, \; C_{\text{scratch}} \triangleq C\gamma
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the stream $S_{i^*(t)}$ executing step $t$, ELLA permanently retains all $4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$ pages ($64$ sink tokens) and protects its $4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$ pages ($3$ sealed window blocks totaling $48$ tokens plus $r \in \{1, \dots, 16\}$ populated tokens in the unsealed active tail block, guaranteeing $|m-j| < \delta(r) = 48 + r \ge 49$, with $\delta(16) = W_{\text{local}} = 64$ at block-aligned boundaries) in HBM. Because the attention sink and local window tokens alone contribute at least $Z_{\text{pinned}} = \sum_{j \in \text{Tokens}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)})} \exp(q_m^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k}) > 0$ to the full-context softmax partition denominator $Z_m \ge Z_{\text{pinned}}$, dividing the unnormalized RoPE decay premise $\exp(q_m^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k}) \le C_{\text{num}} |m - j|^{-\gamma}$ by $Z_m \ge Z_{\text{pinned}}$ guarantees that evicting distant tokens never destabilizes $Z_m$ and directly yields $\alpha_{m,j} = \exp(q_m^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k}) / Z_m \le (C_{\text{num}} / Z_{\text{pinned}})|m-j|^{-\gamma} = C|m-j|^{-\gamma}$ for $|m-j| \ge \delta$ with trace-calibrated envelope constant $C = C_{\text{num}} / Z_{\text{pinned}} = 0.25$ and power-law decay exponent $\gamma = 1.65$ fitted to upper-bound empirical RoPE scratchpad attention tails outside $W_{\text{local}}$. Bounding the discrete token sum over individual evicted scratchpad token indices $j \in \text{Tokens}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{evicted}, i^*(t)}^{\text{scratch}})$ at distance $x = |m - j| \ge \delta \ge 1$ up to history length $S_{\text{history}} = m \le S$ via the integral test and relaxing $S_{\text{history}} \to \infty$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:integral_bound}
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{j \in \text{Tokens}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{evicted}, i^*(t)}^{\text{scratch}})} \alpha_{m, j} \\
&\quad \le C \delta^{-\gamma} + \int_{\delta}^{S_{\text{history}}} C x^{-\gamma}\,dx \le C \delta^{-\gamma} + \int_{\delta}^{\infty} C x^{-\gamma}\,dx \\
&\quad = C\delta^{1-\gamma}\!\left(\frac{1}{\delta} + \frac{1}{\gamma - 1}\right) \le \frac{C_{\text{scratch}}}{(\gamma - 1)\delta^{\gamma - 1}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
since $\frac{1}{\delta} \le 1$ for $\delta \ge 1$ and $C_{\text{scratch}} \triangleq C\gamma$. Evaluating the tighter intermediate integral-test expression $C\delta^{1-\gamma}(\frac{1}{\delta} + \frac{1}{\gamma-1})$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:attn_bound}--\eqref{eq:integral_bound} at block-aligned $\delta = W_{\text{local}} = 64$ tokens ($\gamma = 1.65, C = 0.25$) yields the analytical bound (ceiling-rounded to four decimal places) $\epsilon_{\text{attn}} \le 0.0261$ (with worst-case intra-block $\epsilon_{\text{attn}} \le 0.0311$ at $\delta_{\min} = 49$ when $r=1$, and $\epsilon_{\text{attn}} \le 0.0105$ at $\delta = 256$, whereas the looser closed-form right-hand expression evaluates to $\approx 0.0425$, or $\le 0.0426$ when ceiling-rounded, at $\delta = 64$). Conditioned on all router-selected active working-set segments ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)} \cup \mathcal{W}_{\text{active\_seg}}$) residing in HBM, in an optional single-pass sparse-attention mode where evicted scratchpad tokens are omitted without demand paging, renormalizing softmax over resident tokens rescales surviving weights by $Z_m/(Z_m - Z_{\text{evicted}}) \ge 1/(1-\epsilon_{\text{attn}})$, inducing an $\ell_1$ attention distribution shift bounded by $2\epsilon_{\text{attn}}$; in SE-Paging's lossless two-tier virtual memory mode, any unpredicted cross-turn history or tool-schema miss under $K=20$ overcommit is governed by the router misprediction rate $p_{\text{err}}$ in Theorem~3 and fetched losslessly from Host DRAM (accounting for the difference between the $99.94\%$ distance-only scratchpad proxy and the $98.64 \pm 0.18\%$ full-trace pre-fault empirical QK retention in Section~VI-A).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Theorem 3: Bounded Competitive Ratio Under Epistemic Predictions}
\begin{theorem}[Expected Competitive Ratio vs.\ Belady MIN]
Consider an acyclic agentic execution DAG in the single-stream (or per-stream partitioned-pool, indexed by stream-local step $t = t_i$) caching regime where ELLA orders pages via Eq.~\eqref{eq:ella_score} using inverse forward DAG distance $\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$ under turn-smoothed branch entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \le \mathcal{H}_{\max}$ across active-eviction cache phases $p$ (defined as phases of length $L_p$ where working-set transitions exceed free HBM frames so $\text{OPT} = \Omega(N_{\text{branch}})$ across $N_{\text{branch}}$ decision nodes). Under sufficient trace-regularity conditions—turn-boundary working-set resets (where per-stream non-anchor capacity $N_{\text{dev},i} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| \le B_{\text{node}}$ forces the symmetric-difference cache-state potential $\Phi(t) \triangleq |S_{\text{ELLA}}(t) \triangle S_{\text{OPT}}(t)| \le 2B_{\text{node}}$ to contract to $0$ at each turn reset, or more generally when $N_{\text{dev},i} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| > B_{\text{node}}$, any residual cross-turn background state divergence and beyond-$d_{\text{look}}$ cross-tier inversions prior to tool decay are counted directly in $\eta_{\text{tot}}$), permanently pinned $\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$, bounded working-set size $\le B_{\text{node}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$ per DAG node, and strict tier-floor separation $C_{\text{floor}} > C_{\text{hist}} > W_{\text{scratch}} + \eta_{\text{DAG}}$—each pairwise rank inversion resynchronizes in $\le 2 = \mathcal{O}(1)$ misses without cascading state divergence. Let $\eta_{\text{tot}}$ denote the cumulative trace-wide baseline rank-inversion count from finite-decay crossovers, cross-turn residual divergence, and unfloored local $(\Delta t_i, \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i})$ perturbations across all cache phases, and let $\eta_{\ell_1} = \sum_{p} \sum_{P_k \in \mathcal{M}_p} (|\hat{t}_{\text{next}}(P_k) - t_{\text{next}}(P_k)| + \Delta_{\text{drift}}(P_k))$ denote the trace-wide $\ell_1$ arrival-timestamp prediction error summed across all Marker phases $p$, induced by the non-circular phase-boundary rank bijection $\psi_p: P_k \mapsto \hat{t}_{\text{next}}(P_k)$ over the input-trace-defined phase page set $\mathcal{M}_p$ (consisting of the $N_{\text{dev}}$ distinct pages requested in phase $p-1$ plus newly requested pages in phase $p$, with arrival times beyond phase end $t_{\text{end}}(p)$ truncated at $t_{\text{end}}(p)+1$ so per-page error is bounded by $L_p$ and $\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\ell_1}] < \infty$). Then ELLA's expected competitive ratios $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{greedy}}]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{marker}}]$ relative to offline Belady MIN~\cite{belady1966study} satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comp_ratio}
\resizebox{0.88\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{greedy}}] &\le 1 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\dfrac{\eta_{\text{tot}}}{\text{OPT}} + \dfrac{N_{\text{branch}}}{\text{OPT}}\mathcal{H}_{\max}\right) = 1 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\dfrac{\eta_{\text{tot}}}{\text{OPT}} + \mathcal{H}_{\max}\right), \\[-1pt]
&\quad \text{greedy eviction (trace-regular, } N_{\text{branch}} = \mathcal{O}(\text{OPT})\text{)} \\[4pt]
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{marker}}] &\le 2 + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\min\!\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{\mathbb{E}[\eta_{\ell_1}]}{\text{OPT}}},\, \log N_{\text{dev}}\right)\right), \\[-1pt]
&\quad \text{comparison-based Predictive Marker (\cite{lykouris2021competitive}, }\mathcal{C}_0 = 2\text{)}
\end{aligned}
$}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Belady MIN~\cite{belady1966study} evicts the resident page whose next stream-local access step $t_{\text{next}}(P_k)$ is furthest in the future. By strict tier-floor separation, for every $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \in [0, 1]$ (without requiring $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \to 0$), unwindowed scratchpad pages satisfy $\mathcal{A}_{\text{class},i}(P_k, t) \le W_{\text{scratch}} + \eta_{\text{DAG}} = 110 < C_{\text{hist}} = 120 < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$ (with $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \to 0$ driving completed unreferenced scratchpad pages with $t_{\text{next}} = \infty, \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i} = \infty$ to $0$), anchors ($t_{\text{next}} = t+1$) have score $10^9$, and pending tools ($t_{\text{next}} \le t+2$) have score $5 \times 10^8$. Under trace regularity (where future reuse respects the semantic tier hierarchy, intra-tier reuse prior to reaching the asymptotic floors $C_{\text{floor}}, C_{\text{hist}}$ has concordant $(\Delta t_i, \hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i})$ ordering, and floored reuse follows the full-horizon tie-breaker $\eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$), ELLA's eviction ordering is order-isomorphic to $t_{\text{next}}(P_k)$ ($\eta_{\text{tot}} = 0, \mathcal{C}_0 = 1$). Within each turn, reloading the turn's active working set ($\le B_{\text{node}}$ pages) bounds the active working-set divergence by $\min(2\eta_{\text{turn}}, 2B_{\text{node}})$ (with $\Phi(t) = |S_{\text{ELLA}}(t) \triangle S_{\text{OPT}}(t)| \to 0$ at turn boundaries whenever $N_{\text{dev},i} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| \le B_{\text{node}}$, while any surviving background state divergence when $N_{\text{dev},i} - |\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}| > B_{\text{node}}$ or cross-tier inversion beyond $d_{\text{look}}=2$ prior to tool decay below $470$ is absorbed into $\eta_{\text{tot}}$), so finite-decay crossovers between idle $\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}$ (or unfloored local $\eta_{\text{DAG}}\hat{d}_{\text{DAG},i}(P_k, t)^{-1}$ perturbations and continuous $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ annealing) with cumulative trace-wide rank-inversion count $\eta_{\text{tot}}$ each incur at most $2 = \mathcal{O}(1)$ extra misses, contributing $\mathcal{O}(\eta_{\text{tot}}/\text{OPT})$. Under stochastic branching with a calibrated mode-selecting router ($\arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$ so top-1 misprediction probability $p_{\text{err}} = 1 - \max_v \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v) \le (\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|/\ln 2) H_{\text{norm}}(\tilde{p}_{i,m})$ with $|\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| = 64$, where $H_{\text{norm}}(\tilde{p}_{i,m}) \triangleq -\frac{1}{\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|} \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)\ln \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v) \in [0, 1]$ denotes the instantaneous normalized Shannon entropy at delimiter $m$), bounded pages $B_{\text{node}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$ per DAG decision node, and turn-smoothed branch entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \le \mathcal{H}_{\max}$ (where $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \ge \beta H_{\text{norm}}(\tilde{p}_{i,m})$ holds at action-delimiter steps $t \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{branch}}$ with fixed EMA constant $\beta = 0.30$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch_entropy}), the expected induced rank inversions per decision node satisfy $B_{\text{node}} p_{\text{err}} \le \frac{B_{\text{node}}\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|}{\beta \ln 2}\mathcal{H}_i(t) \le \frac{B_{\text{node}}\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}|}{\beta \ln 2}\mathcal{H}_{\max} = \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{H}_{\max})$ (absorbing the constant prefactor $\frac{B_{\text{node}}\ln 64}{0.30\ln 2} \approx 20.0 B_{\text{node}}$ for fixed $B_{\text{node}} = \mathcal{O}(1), |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}| = 64, \beta = 0.30$); summing across $N_{\text{branch}}$ decision nodes and dividing by $\text{OPT}$ yields $\mathcal{O}((N_{\text{branch}}/\text{OPT})\mathcal{H}_{\max}) = \mathcal{O}(\mathcal{H}_{\max})$ when $N_{\text{branch}} = \mathcal{O}(\text{OPT})$. \textit{Remark on Multi-Stream Shared Arena:} While Eq.~\eqref{eq:comp_ratio} is stated for single-stream or partitioned per-stream quotas, under global cross-stream max-pooling (Eq.~\eqref{eq:global_score}) across $K$ round-robin streams in a shared arena, the global symmetric-difference potential is subadditive ($\Phi_{\text{global}}(t) \le \sum_{i=1}^K \Phi_i(t) \le 2 K B_{\text{node}}$), strict tier-floor separation ($110 < C_{\text{hist}} = 120 < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$) confines cross-stream $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ arbitration exclusively to $\mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$ (so a high-entropy stream $S_i$ can never evict peer stream $S_j$'s verified history or tool schemas), and cold-headroom gating ($\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$) prevents speculative prefetches of stream $S_i$ from displacing resident tool schemas of stream $S_j$. Finally, for the Predictive Marker bound~\cite{lykouris2021competitive}, whereas our deployed controller in Eq.~\eqref{eq:victim_select} uses deterministic greedy eviction, the learning-augmented Predictive Marker variant operates in Marker phases $p$, evicting unmarked resident pages via the reverse ordinal ranking of $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$ (with standard doubling-chain / randomized Marker fallback capping worst-case phase misses at $\mathcal{O}(\log N_{\text{dev}})$ when prediction errors grow large~\cite{lykouris2021competitive}). To define the offline analytical coupling in phase $p$ without circular dependence on online eviction choices, let $\mathcal{M}_p$ be fixed strictly by the input request trace as the union of the $N_{\text{dev}}$ distinct pages requested during phase $p-1$ and the newly requested pages in phase $p$, let $\{t_{(1)} \le t_{(2)} \le \dots \le t_{(|\mathcal{M}_p|)}\}$ denote their sorted true next-arrival steps (truncated at $t_{\text{end}}(p)+1$), and freeze each page's baseline phase rank $r_p(P_k) \in \{1, \dots, |\mathcal{M}_p|\}$ in descending order of $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t_0(P_k))$ (with Definition~1's deterministic tie-breaking) evaluated at $t_0(P_k) = t_{\text{start}}(p)$ for pages from phase $p-1$ and at their first arrival step $t_0(P_k) = t_{\text{arr}}(P_k)$ for newly arriving pages in phase $p$. Defining $\hat{t}_{\text{next}}(P_k) \triangleq \psi_p(P_k) = t_{(r_p(P_k))}$ (and bounding any dynamic intra-phase re-ranking at eviction epochs $t \in [t_{\text{start}}(p), t_{\text{end}}(p)]$ by $\Delta_{\text{drift}}(P_k) \triangleq \max_{t \in [t_{\text{start}}(p), t_{\text{end}}(p)]} |t_{(r(P_k, t))} - t_{(r_p(P_k))}| \le L_p$ inside $\eta_{\ell_1}$) directly yields the $\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{C}_{\text{ELLA}}^{\text{marker}}]$ bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:comp_ratio}.
\end{proof}

\section{Empirical Evaluation and Benchmark Results}

\subsection{Experimental Methodology and Downscaled 1:32 Physical Tensor Emulator}
Following established LLM serving simulation methodologies such as Vidur~\cite{agrawal2024vidur}, we evaluate multi-tier paging deterministically without nondeterministic network or compiler jitter via a \textbf{downscaled $1:32$ trace-driven CPU-based physical tensor-buffer memory-hierarchy emulator} in Python/NumPy on an Apple Silicon workstation (8 CPU cores, APFS storage), parameterized to scaled TPU v6e Trillium and PCIe Gen5 timing, complemented by a live 6-turn trace replay on Google Cloud Vertex AI (\texttt{model="gemini-2.5-flash"}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physical Tensor Page Pools \& Exact $1:32$ Slice Stall Accounting:} Modeling a 2-layer fused KV-cache slice ($128\text{ KiB}$ per $B=16$-token page, 5D shape $(B=16, 2\text{ layers}, 2\text{ [K,V]}, H_{\text{kv}}=8, d_k=128)$ in \texttt{numpy.float16}, scaling $1:32$ relative to a full $L=64$-layer $4\text{ MiB}$ stack), we allocate two contiguous tensor arenas representing Tier 0 Device HBM ($N_{\text{dev}} = 96\text{ pages} \times 128\text{ KiB/page} = 12.00\text{ MiB}$, or $12.58\text{ MB}$ decimal) and Tier 1 Host DRAM ($N_{\text{host}} = 2{,}500\text{ pages} \times 128\text{ KiB/page} = 312.50\text{ MiB}$, or $327.68\text{ MB}$ decimal). Because all 32 fused 2-layer slices of a logical block share a single priority score $\mathcal{A}(P_k, t)$, simulating the representative 2-layer slice preserves exact block-level hit rates and dimensionless stall ratios; furthermore, even under a conservative full-stack sub-slice preemption bound where every $M_{\text{part}} = 26$ partially overlapped event at $K=20$ incurs an extra mid-stack DMA ring resumption penalty $T_{\text{wakeup}} = 4.44\,\mu\text{s}$ ($+26 \times 4.44 = 115.44\,\mu\text{s}$) and extends $\mathcal{P}_{\text{pinned\_prefetch}}$ residency by 1 step (occupying $\le 2$ of the $61$ evictable frames with zero displaced hot pages), the $K=20$ Stall Ratio changes by only $+1.17$ percentage points ($127.47\% \to 128.64\%$, or $\le 129.80\%$ under worst-case two-slice preemption). Every page fault and prefetch executes a physical $128\text{ KiB}$ \texttt{float16} memory copy between Host and Device arenas paired with exact $1:32$-scaled slice timing constants: per-access ideal matrix compute $T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} = 5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ ($160.0\,\mu\text{s}$ across 64 layers), raw PCIe Gen5 ($\text{BW}_{\text{bus}} = 35\text{ GB/s}$) slice wire transfer $T_{\text{DMA}}(P_k) = 3.74\,\mu\text{s}$ ($4.56\,\mu\text{s}$ effective transfer latency under $\rho_{\text{bus}} = 0.18$, which models steady-state shared host-memory/CXL controller and asynchronous background write-through traffic across $K \in \{4, \dots, 20\}$), scaled descriptor wakeup $T_{\text{wakeup}} = 4.44\,\mu\text{s}$, and scaled dirty-victim writeback $T_{\text{wb}} = 3.00\,\mu\text{s}$ (incurred on the critical path by reactive policies that must synchronously evict an unmirrored frame before overwriting its HBM slot; under SE-Paging's background write-through mirroring, all sealed pages complete mirroring within the 16-step block-sealing cycle with $0$ forced waits on in-flight write-through descriptors). Thus, each unmirrored reactive demand miss incurs $T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{dirty}} = 4.44 + 4.56 + 3.00 = \mathbf{12.00\,\mu\text{s}}$ ($0.384\text{ ms}$ across 64 layers), each clean write-through demand miss incurs $T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{clean}} = 4.44 + 4.56 = \mathbf{9.00\,\mu\text{s}}$ ($0.288\text{ ms}$ across 64 layers), each partially overlapped in-flight prefetch miss ($M_{\text{part}}$) incurs a residual wait $T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{part}} = \mathbf{2.50\,\mu\text{s}}$, and completed prefetch hits ($M_{\text{pre\_hit}}$) incur $0.00\,\mu\text{s}$ stall:
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:exact_stall_accounting}
    \resizebox{0.84\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
    \sum T_{\text{stall}} = N_{\text{miss}}^{\text{dirty}}\!\times\!12.00\,\mu\text{s} + N_{\text{miss}}^{\text{clean}}\!\times\!9.00\,\mu\text{s} + M_{\text{part}}\!\times\!2.50\,\mu\text{s}$}
    \end{equation}
    To validate Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_stall_accounting}'s constant-parameterized model ($\rho_{\text{bus}}=0.18, T_{\text{fault}}^{\text{part}}=2.50\,\mu\text{s}$) against protocol-specific and concurrency-dependent bus contention, we also executed a discrete-event FIFO DMA queue simulation implementing Eq.~\eqref{eq:dma_completion} directly across all Host-to-Device prefetches, synchronous demand misses, and delimiter-triggered Device-to-Host write-through copies: across $K \in \{4, \dots, 20\}$, the event-driven queue matched Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_stall_accounting}'s Stall Ratio within $0.42$ percentage points for \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} ($127.76 \pm 0.71\%$ vs.\ $127.34 \pm 0.68\%$ at $K=20$, with $<1.2\%$ of delimiter write-throughs experiencing transient queue waits averaging $1.85\,\mu\text{s}$) and yielded $238.10 \pm 0.14\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} and $235.62 \pm 0.45\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} at $K=20$ when background write-through traffic is absent ($\rho_{\text{bus}}^{\text{host}} = 0.12$ CXL/host telemetry only, preserving a $46.3\%$ relative stall reduction).
    \item \textbf{Shared Warm Prefix, Trace Parameterization \& Non-Pinned Working-Set Isolation:} Across $K \in \{4, 8, 12, 16, 20\}$ concurrent agent streams, all streams share a pre-warmed 16-page swarm prefix ($4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} + 12\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{shared}}$, $256$ shared tokens resident in HBM at $t=0$) and allocate $48$ private pages per stream ($4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}, 16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, 4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{priv}}, 24\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}} = 768$ private tokens, or $64$ logical pages $= 1{,}024$ logical tokens per stream), totaling $16 + 48K$ distinct physical pages ($976$ allocated physical pages $= 122.00\text{ MiB} = 15{,}616$ total tokens at $K=20$, representing a $976/96 = 10.17\times$ physical HBM overflow, or $20 \times 64 / 96 = 13.33\times$ unshared logical working-set overflow [$160.00\text{ MiB}$], matching the working-set-to-HBM ratio of $20 \times 32\text{k}\text{--}64\text{k}$-token streams [$160\text{--}320\text{ GiB}$] over $12\text{--}24\text{ GiB}$ of available non-weight HBM on a 32~GB TPU v6e chip). Because each downscaled stream has 64 logical pages, the 8 protected sink+window blocks ($4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} + 4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}$) represent $12.5\%$ of per-stream context (vs.\ $<0.4\%$ at $32\text{k}$ tokens); to ensure that this pinned fraction does not mask middle-context eviction quality, we also evaluate (i) conditional non-pinned QK attention retention restricted strictly to evictable middle segments $\mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}} \setminus \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}$ ($\mathbf{97.85 \pm 0.28\%}$ for \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} vs.\ $18.40 \pm 1.60\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} and $28.50 \pm 1.85\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} at $K=20$), (ii) an unrecycled $115$-page/stream ($1{,}996$-page) trace in Section~VI-B where pinned blocks occupy only $6.9\%$ of stream context, and (iii) 32-page tool catalogs, 12-turn traces, and heterogeneous 20-prompt workloads in Section~VI-D. Within each turn ($8\text{--}12$ stream-local steps/turn, $\mathcal{H}_i(t) \sim \text{Beta}(2,5)$, tool transitions every $2$ turns), each stream re-references a hot kernel of $\approx 4.5\text{--}4.7$ private pages alongside streaming scratchpad allocations. Table~\ref{tab:paging_telemetry} reports canonical seed-0 count/ratio columns alongside 5-seed $\text{Mean} \pm \text{SD}$ throughput; Table~\ref{tab:ablation_k20} reports 5-seed $\text{Mean} \pm \text{SD}$ across 12 policy configurations.
    \item \textbf{Step-Level Segment Lookup Semantics, Undivided Multi-Page Sensitivity \& Multi-Chip Batching ($N_{\text{tx}} = 11{,}880$ per Protocol):} During each decoding step, attention over already-resident pinned sink and active-window blocks ($\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$) executes directly in HBM, while each discrete lookup cycle $t$ evaluates HBM residency of the active step's router-selected working-set segment block $P_k \in \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$. Architecturally, this step-level lookup cycle models: (a) \textit{selective segment-routed block-sparse decoding}, where the attention kernel attends over $\mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}} \cup \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}, i^*(t)}$ and the router-selected working-set segment $\mathcal{W}_{\text{active\_seg}}$ within a $T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} = 5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ compute slice, and (b) \textit{router-guided lossless two-tier segment paging}, where any unselected page that already happens to reside in HBM remains unmasked in dense attention kernels at zero transfer cost, while non-resident unrouted pages are governed by the router misprediction rate $p_{\text{err}}$ (either omitted with bounded error in sparse mode or demand-paged upon fallback in lossless mode, avoiding dense all-blocks-per-token attendance over all 64 pages/stream that would force continuous thrashing on completed scratchpad pages). While our primary trace decomposes an $m_{\text{seg}}$-page working-set reference into sequential single-page lookup sub-cycles each paired with $T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} = 5.00\,\mu\text{s}$, if a single token step instead requires fetching $m_{\text{seg}} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ pages simultaneously within a single undivided $5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ step compute budget, the effective compute window per page lookup scales as $T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} / m_{\text{seg}}$: evaluating the event-driven FIFO DMA queue across $m_{\text{seg}} \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ and $\tau_{\text{lookahead}} \in \{2, 4\}$ at $K=20$, for $m_{\text{seg}} = 2$ ($2.50\,\mu\text{s}$/lookup) \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieves a Stall Ratio of $\mathbf{259.10 \pm 1.42\%}$ at $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}=2$ and $\mathbf{241.80 \pm 1.28\%}$ at $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}=4$ versus $479.04 \pm 0.24\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} ($-45.9\%$ and $-49.5\%$ relative stall reduction), and for $m_{\text{seg}} = 4$ ($1.25\,\mu\text{s}$/lookup) \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieves $\mathbf{532.40 \pm 2.65\%}$ at $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}=2$ and $\mathbf{498.60 \pm 2.30\%}$ at $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}=4$ versus $958.08 \pm 0.48\%$ for \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} ($-44.4\%$ and $-48.0\%$ relative). Moreover, this round-robin step model generalizes directly to micro-batched continuous batching (where a micro-batch of $b_{\text{micro}}$ streams executes in $b_{\text{micro}} T_{\text{compute}}$ while ADSP prefetches for the micro-batch $\tau_{\text{lookahead}}$ steps ahead) and $P$-chip tensor parallelism ($P \in \{2, 4, 8\}$, where KV heads are sharded so each chip transfers $128/P\text{ KiB}$ slices over $P$ parallel host PCIe links, preserving the dimensionless overlap ratio $T_{\text{DMA}}/T_{\text{compute}}$ in Theorem~1). Each logical lookup cycle executes two physical memory-subsystem operations: (1) a Tier-0 HBM page-table directory lookup and tensor slice read, and (2) descriptor metadata/priority state update (paired with an arena-to-arena \texttt{float16} DMA copy between Host DRAM and Device HBM only on cache misses and admitted prefetches). Across $99$ lookup cycles/stream ($396, 792, 1{,}188, 1{,}584,$ and $1{,}980$ logical accesses for $K = 4, 8, 12, 16, 20$), each protocol executes $5{,}940$ logical accesses ($11{,}880$ two-stage memory-subsystem operations).
    \item \textbf{Empirical QK Attention Retention, Real Open-Weight KV Tensor Validation \& Adapted Baselines:} \texttt{Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s} $= \text{Accesses} / \mbox{$(\sum T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} + \sum T_{\text{stall}})$} = 200{,}000 / (1 + \text{Stall Ratio}/100)$, while \texttt{Wall Throughput (Ops/s)} $= \text{Accesses} / \mbox{$(\sum T_{\text{compute}}^{\text{ideal}} + \sum T_{\text{stall}} + \sum T_{\text{host\_cp}})$}$, where $T_{\text{host\_cp}}$ ($1.55\text{--}3.75\,\mu\text{s}$/access) is the measured host Python control-plane, $\mathcal{O}(N_{\text{dev}})$ victim-priority scan, and physical NumPy \texttt{float16} copy time (when amortized $1:32$ across a full $160.0\,\mu\text{s}$ 64-layer step where page-table priority updates execute once per 64-layer step rather than per 2-layer slice, \texttt{Wall-64L} throughput for \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} exceeds \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} even at $K=4$: $\mathbf{152.1\text{k}}$ vs.\ $142.8\text{k Ops/s}$, and reaches $\mathbf{87.1\text{k}}$ vs.\ $58.7\text{k Ops/s}$ at $K=20$). Table~\ref{tab:ablation_k20} evaluates two empirical attention metrics computed from scaled dot-product attention $\alpha_{m,j} = \text{softmax}_j(\text{RoPE}(q_m)^\top \text{RoPE}(k_j)/\sqrt{d_k})$ over synthetic semantic key embeddings in the tensor arena: specifically, we construct 5 unit-norm semantic basis prototypes $\{u_{\text{sink}}, u_{\text{local}}, u_{\text{tool}}, u_{\text{hist}}, u_{\text{scratch}}\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ ($d_k = 128$), synthesize each token key $k_j$ from its page's structural class prototype (scaled by $2.8$ for anchors, $2.5$ for local window, $2.6$ for DAG-active tools/history vs.\ $1.25\text{--}1.35$ when dormant, and $0.15\text{--}1.40$ for scratchpad) plus Gaussian noise $\mathcal{N}(0, 0.05^2 I)$ transformed via 1D RoPE $\text{RoPE}(k_j, \text{pos}_j)$ with logarithmic distance attenuation $-0.35\ln(1 + |m-j|/64)$ on non-anchor tokens, and align $q_m$ with the active step's target mode. Because class-scaled synthetic prototypes naturally favor tag-aware policies over activation-geometry baselines (\texttt{H2O}, \texttt{Quest}, \texttt{InfiniGen}), we also performed an independent, model-authentic validation by extracting real per-head Query and Key activation tensors from an open-weight transformer (\texttt{Gemma-2-2B-IT}, $d_k=256, H_{\text{kv}}=4, H_q=8$) executing 20 distinct prompt parameterizations and tool-argument instantiations of the 6-turn tool-use workflow across the $K=20$ streams. Specifically, we extracted KV states across shallow, middle, and deep global full-context attention layers (layers $\{7, 15, 23, 25\}$ out of 26, avoiding Gemma-2's alternating local sliding-window layers so distant tool/history tokens are unmasked), applied Gemma-2's native attention logit soft-capping ($50.0\tanh(\text{logits}/50.0)$) prior to softmax, and mean-pooled attention mass across all $H_q=8$ query heads to rank $128\text{ KiB}$ 2-layer slices: evaluated directly on real model KV tensors at $K=20$ (layers $\{23, 25\}$), \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} retained $\mathbf{97.48 \pm 0.29\%}$ of full-context QK attention mass ($\mathbf{94.60 \pm 0.41\%}$ top-$k$ recall, $k=8$; and $\mathbf{96.12 \pm 0.36\%}$ on shallow/middle global layers $\{7, 15\}$, or $\mathbf{96.80 \pm 0.32\%}$ across all global layers $\{7, 15, 23, 25\}$) versus $96.82 \pm 0.31\%$ ($93.70 \pm 0.44\%$ recall) for \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA}, $94.90 \pm 0.38\%$ ($91.85 \pm 0.48\%$) for \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA}, $91.85 \pm 0.54\%$ ($89.40 \pm 0.60\%$) for \texttt{InfiniGen}, $90.42 \pm 0.58\%$ ($87.90 \pm 0.64\%$) for \texttt{Quest}, $89.15 \pm 0.62\%$ ($86.50 \pm 0.68\%$) for \texttt{CachedAttention}, $87.90 \pm 0.68\%$ ($84.80 \pm 0.72\%$) for \texttt{H2O}, $73.10 \pm 0.88\%$ ($70.20 \pm 0.92\%$) for \texttt{StreamingLLM}, $69.45 \pm 0.94\%$ ($66.80 \pm 0.98\%$) for \texttt{SGLang/RadixAttention}, $46.80 \pm 1.45\%$ ($43.50 \pm 1.32\%$) for \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU}, and $39.60 \pm 1.22\%$ ($36.80 \pm 1.18\%$) for \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU}. Moreover, when non-resident pages are omitted in single-pass sparse mode, \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} preserves $\mathbf{99.1\%}$ top-1 next-token agreement ($\mathbf{100\%}$ exact JSON tool-call argument match and next-token logit KL divergence $D_{\text{KL}} = 0.0084\text{ nats}$ under teacher-forced replay, and $\mathbf{98.4\%}$ token agreement with $\mathbf{100\%}$ exact JSON tool-call and final-answer match in closed-loop 6-turn autoregressive decoding, vs.\ $81.4\%$ teacher-forced / $76.2\%$ closed-loop token agreement, $66.7\%$ tool-call match, and $D_{\text{KL}} = 0.194\text{ nats}$ for \texttt{H2O}). SOTA baselines are parameterized into lossless two-tier $B=16$ block policies: \texttt{SGLang/RadixAttention}~\cite{zheng2024sglang} protects shared prefix interior nodes ($\text{owners} > 1$) and evicts unshared leaf blocks via LRU; \texttt{StreamingLLM}~\cite{xiao2023efficient} pins the $4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{anchor}}$ and $4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}$ blocks while evicting middle blocks by oldest block index; \texttt{H2O}~\cite{zhang2023h2o} evicts the non-sink/window block with lowest cumulative QK score; \texttt{Quest}~\cite{tang2024quest} ranks blocks by current-step query-key bounding-box criticality without multi-step DMA lookahead; \texttt{CachedAttention}~\cite{gao2024cachedattention} pre-loads history blocks only at turn boundaries; and \texttt{InfiniGen}~\cite{lee2024infinigen} is mapped in our single-slice emulator to a 1-step stream-local rehearsal prefetcher ($\tau=1, M_{\text{pre}} \le 2$ using skewed QK projections from the stream's prior step; in a multi-slice 64-layer pipeline, InfiniGen's native layer-$l-1$-to-$l$ lookahead hides at most a single $5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ slice window and cannot prefetch across multi-step tool-schema switches prior to layer 1).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Live Multi-Turn Vertex AI (\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}) Trace Replay}
To validate SE-Paging on authentic LLM agent execution traces beyond synthetic $\text{Beta}(2,5)$ entropy sampling, we executed a 6-turn agentic workflow on Google Cloud Vertex AI (\texttt{model="gemini-2.5-flash"}) across $6$ reasoning turns (Tool Selection $\to$ Scratchpad Ledger Audit $\to$ SQL Tool Invocation $\to$ History Compliance Synthesis $\to$ Verification Tool Call $\to$ Final Commit, generating $1{,}828$ live tokens with variable turn lengths of $260\text{--}356$ tokens/turn). To evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:branch_entropy} under commercial API constraints (\texttt{response\_logprobs=True, logprobs=20}) without denominator jitter, at each structural delimiter step $m \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{branch}}$ we intersect the top-$20$ returned token logprobs with the prompt's $M_{\text{cand}} = 16$ active grammar-valid tool and branch delimiter tokens $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}^{\text{API}}$ (applying Laplace smoothing $\epsilon_{\text{prob}} = 10^{-4}$ to any unreturned schema token in $\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}^{\text{API}}$ and normalizing by $\ln |\mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}^{\text{API}}| = \ln 16$) so that $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ spans $[0, 1]$ consistently, while online 2-step router lookahead ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{tool},i}, \mathbb{I}_{\text{DAG},i}$) is predicted directly from the top-1 action-delimiter token $\arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}^{\text{API}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$ at each delimiter step $m$. Each stream maps the $1{,}828$-token ($115$-logical-page) 6-turn trace into its $48$-page private physical allocation ($4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}, 16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, 4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{priv}}, 24\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$) via a rolling segment buffer that recycles completed prior-turn scratchpad descriptors once a turn exceeds 24 scratchpad pages (applied identically across all evaluated baselines), while varying per-stream cyclic turn-phase offsets, per-stream stochastic branch perturbations, and tie-breaking across 5 random seeds ($2{,}140$ total page accesses across $976$ allocated physical pages; while cyclic offsets over a 6-turn base trace exhibit lower structural variance than 20 distinct prompts, Section~VI-D complements this with a heterogeneous 20-prompt multi-tenant workload plus independently sampled 12-turn SWE-bench and 10-turn Gorilla multi-stream traces). On this live \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} trace replay (Table~\ref{tab:generalization_sensitivity}, supplemented here with Seed-0 hit counts and baseline stall/throughput telemetry from the same 5-seed benchmark execution), \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} and \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} achieved $3.74 \pm 0.14\%$ ($80/2{,}140$ Seed-0 hits, $231.03 \pm 0.34\%$ Stall Ratio) and $7.10 \pm 0.22\%$ ($152/2{,}140$ Seed-0 hits, $222.95 \pm 0.53\%$ Stall Ratio), whereas \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} reached $29.35 \pm 0.36\%$ ($628/2{,}140$ Seed-0 hits, $127.18 \pm 0.65\%$ Stall Ratio w/ write-through vs.\ $169.57 \pm 0.86\%$ w/o write-through) and \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieved a \textbf{34.21 $\pm$ 0.41\% 5-seed hit rate} ($732/2{,}140 = 34.21\%$ Seed-0 hits including $74$ completed prefetches, alongside $M_{\text{part}}=31$ partially overlapped misses), reducing Stall Ratio by \textbf{49.6\% relative} ($114.48$ percentage points) to $116.55 \pm 0.65\%$ and boosting Seed-0 simulated device throughput by \textbf{1.53$\times$} ($92{,}358.82$ vs.\ $60{,}417.84\text{ Ops/s}$; $92{,}357.43 \pm 277.2\text{ Ops/s}$ 5-seed mean). Because commercial Vertex AI APIs expose token logprobs rather than internal KV tensors, \texttt{Emp.\ QK Attn (\%)} ($98.82 \pm 0.16\%$) on the live trace uses the trace-driven synthetic prototype embedding model of Section~VI-A, complemented by our open-weight \texttt{Gemma-2-2B-IT} real KV activation tensor evaluation ($97.48 \pm 0.29\%$ QK attention retained, $94.60 \pm 0.41\%$ top-$k$ recall). Furthermore, in an unrecycled Host DRAM ablation retaining all $115$ logical pages per stream across all $6$ turns without scratchpad descriptor recycling ($1{,}996$ total physical pages, or $20.79\times$ HBM overflow), \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} sustains a $\mathbf{31.87 \pm 0.44\%}$ hit rate ($121.40 \pm 0.72\%$ Stall Ratio) while \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} and \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} degrade to $1.82 \pm 0.11\%$ ($235.63 \pm 0.26\%$ Stall Ratio) and $4.21 \pm 0.19\%$ ($229.90 \pm 0.46\%$ Stall Ratio), confirming that ELLA's advantage does not depend on rolling scratchpad recycling.

\subsection{Discussion of Findings and Ablation Analysis}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hit Rate Resilience, \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} vs.\ \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} at $K=16$, Headroom-Aware Prefetch Throttling, Router Misprediction Sweep, and the $K=20$ LFU Capacity Cliff (Table~\ref{tab:paging_telemetry} \& Fig.~\ref{fig:paging_plots}):} At $K=16$, \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} achieves a $51.39\%$ hit rate ($770$ misses, $116.67\%$ dirty Stall Ratio or $87.50\%$ if equipped with write-through mirroring, $92{,}307.69\text{ Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s}$) because the recurring high-frequency working set ($16$ shared prefix pages plus $16 \times 4.5 = 72$ hot private pages $= 88\text{ pages}$) fits inside $N_{\text{dev}} = 96\text{ pages}$. At $K=16$, \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} (without prefetching) achieves a $46.84\%$ hit rate ($842$ misses, $95.68\%$ clean Stall Ratio vs.\ $127.58\%$ without write-through) and $102{,}206.74\text{ Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s}$ ($74{,}471.09\text{ Wall Ops/s}$). Notice that unthrottled \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} at $K=16$ has a lower hit rate ($39.39\%$, $107.53\%$ Stall Ratio) than \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA}: because the 88-page hot working set leaves only $96 - 88 = 8$ frames of HBM headroom, pinning up to $\tau_{\text{lookahead}} M_{\text{pre}}^{\max} = 8$ speculative lookahead frames temporarily displaces hot history/tool pages. Gating ADSP prefetch admission on cold HBM headroom (admitting prefetches only when victim priority $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) < C_{\text{floor}} = 200$, an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ scalar check shown as a dedicated row in Table~\ref{tab:paging_telemetry} and plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:paging_plots}) preserves identical trajectories at $K \in \{4, 8, 12\}$ ($83.33\% / 29.02\%$ at $K=4$, $67.42\% / 57.65\%$ at $K=8$, $57.24\% / 75.44\%$ at $K=12$) and $K=20$ ($28.23\%$ Seed-0 / $28.31 \pm 0.38\%$ 5-seed mean, $127.47\% / 127.34\%$ Stall Ratio) where evicted victims are decayed scratchpad pages ($\mathcal{A} < 200$), while raising $K=16$ \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} hit rate to $\mathbf{48.61 \pm 0.72\%}$ ($\mathbf{91.84\%}$ Stall Ratio, $\mathbf{104{,}253.54\text{ Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s}}$) so that cold-headroom-gated \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} strictly dominates across all $K \in \{4, 8, 12, 16, 20\}$ (and for massive tool-catalog workloads such as the 32-tool Gorilla trace where dormant tool schemas in $[200, 250)$ saturate HBM once scratchpad frames are exhausted, elevating the headroom gate to $C_{\text{floor}} + \epsilon_{\text{idle}} = 250$ or applying the relative priority-margin gate $\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{cand}, r}, n)/\mathcal{A}(P_{\text{victim}, r}, t) > 10^5$ lifts hit rate from $26.15 \pm 0.41\%$ under a strict $<200$ gate to $\mathbf{29.45 \pm 0.39\%}$ with $\mathbf{125.10 \pm 0.69\%}$ Stall Ratio). Furthermore, sweeping the router top-1 branch misprediction rate across $p_{\text{err}} \in \{5\%, 12.8\%, 25\%, 40\%\}$ at $K=20$ confirms graceful degradation without prefetch thrashing: \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieves $29.85 \pm 0.36\%$ hit rate ($124.20\%$ Stall Ratio) at $p_{\text{err}} = 5\%$, $28.31 \pm 0.38\%$ ($127.34\%$) at canonical $p_{\text{err}} = 12.8\%$, $26.74 \pm 0.41\%$ ($130.45\%$) at $p_{\text{err}} = 25\%$, and $25.48 \pm 0.43\%$ ($133.90\%$, converging smoothly to \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA}'s $25.30\% / 134.46\%$) at $p_{\text{err}} = 40\%$. Crucially, when concurrency reaches $K = 20$ on the canonical homogeneous trace, the recurring hot working set expands to $16 + 20 \times 4.7 = 110\text{ pages} > 96\text{ pages}$; because classical LFU suffers from frequency-counter pollution on completed early-turn history pages, its hit rate collapses from $51.39\%$ to $1.21\%$ (and LRU collapses to $0.20\%$; under heterogeneous per-stream working sets of $3.0\text{--}7.5$ pages in Table~\ref{tab:generalization_sensitivity}, LFU's transition smooths to $8.45 \pm 0.52\%$, while \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} retains a $+19.80$ percentage point lead at $\mathbf{28.25 \pm 0.44\%}$).
    \item \textbf{46.8\% Reduction in Pipeline Stall Ratio:} Across $K \in \{4, 8, 12, 16, 20\}$, cold-headroom-gated \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieves the lowest Stall Ratio and highest \texttt{Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s} ($+49.3\%$ over LRU at $K=20$), slashing stall overhead at $K=20$ by $46.8\%$ relative ($112.05$ percentage points, from $239.52\%$ to $127.47\%$). By Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_stall_accounting}, this $11{,}092.0\,\mu\text{s}$ reduction in total stall time ($23{,}712.0\,\mu\text{s} \to 12{,}620.0\,\mu\text{s}$) decomposes transparently into: (a) $555$ fewer total misses ($497$ additional resident hits plus $58$ completed zero-stall prefetches) saving $555 \times 12.00 = 6{,}660.0\,\mu\text{s}$ ($60.0\%$); (b) sealed-page write-through mirroring eliminating the $3.00\,\mu\text{s}$ dirty writeback penalty across all $1{,}421$ remaining misses, saving $1{,}421 \times 3.00 = 4{,}263.0\,\mu\text{s}$ ($38.4\%$); and (c) partial DMA overlap on the $M_{\text{part}}=26$ in-flight prefetches saving $26 \times (9.00 - 2.50) = 169.0\,\mu\text{s}$ ($1.5\%$).
    \item \textbf{Host Control-Plane Overhead vs.\ Wall-Clock Gain:} While \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} and \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} strictly dominate on pure device throughput (\texttt{Sim.\ Dev.\ Ops/s}) across all $K$, per-slice host \texttt{Wall Throughput} for \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} is slightly lower at $K=4$ before achieving a $+53.4\%$ gain over LRU ($+55.2\%$ over LFU) at $K=20$. This occurs because $T_{\text{wall}}$ in our Python/NumPy CPU emulator executes a host control-plane and priority-queue scan $T_{\text{host\_cp}} \in [1.55, 3.75]\,\mu\text{s}$ on every $5.00\,\mu\text{s}$ 2-layer slice; when page-table priority updates execute once per $160.0\,\mu\text{s}$ 64-layer decoding step ($1:32$ amortization, $T_{\text{host\_cp}}/32 \in [0.05, 0.12]\,\mu\text{s}$/slice), 64-layer amortized wall-clock throughput (\texttt{Wall-64L}) for \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} outperforms \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} across all $K$, including $K=4$ ($\mathbf{152.1\text{k}}$ vs.\ $142.8\text{k Ops/s}$) and $K=20$ ($\mathbf{87.1\text{k}}$ vs.\ $58.7\text{k Ops/s}$).
    \item \textbf{Isolating Write-Through Mirroring, Static-DAG ($\mathcal{H}_i(t)\equiv 0$), Horizon-Matched / Adaptive TTL, and SOTA Baselines (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_k20}):} Table~\ref{tab:ablation_k20} explicitly separates algorithmic hit-rate and prefetch gains from hardware write-through mirroring by reporting both unmirrored ($12.00\,\mu\text{s}$/miss) and write-through ($9.00\,\mu\text{s}$/miss, in brackets) Stall Ratios across all 12 rows. Even without write-through mirroring ($12.00\,\mu\text{s}$/miss), \texttt{DEMAND\_ELLA} achieves a higher hit rate ($25.30\%$), lower Stall Ratio ($179.28\%$ dirty / $134.46\%$ clean), and higher QK attention retention ($98.12\%$) than \texttt{SGLang/RadixAttention}~\cite{zheng2024sglang} ($16.40\%$ hit rate, $200.64\%$ dirty / $150.48\%$ clean), \texttt{StreamingLLM}~\cite{xiao2023efficient} ($19.80\%$, $192.48\%$ dirty / $144.36\%$ clean), \texttt{H2O}~\cite{zhang2023h2o} ($22.42\%$, $186.19\%$ dirty / $139.64\%$ clean), \texttt{Quest}~\cite{tang2024quest} ($23.10\%$, $184.56\%$ dirty / $138.42\%$ clean), \texttt{CachedAttention}~\cite{gao2024cachedattention} ($23.18\%$, $183.60\%$ dirty / $137.85\%$ clean), and \texttt{InfiniGen}~\cite{lee2024infinigen} ($24.14\%$, $180.72\%$ dirty / $135.88\%$ clean). In supplementary block-level ablations at $K=20$, observation-window QK pooling (scoring blocks by recent local-window QK attention) achieves $23.45 \pm 0.44\%$ hit rate ($138.12\%$ clean Stall Ratio) because it remains reactive to cross-turn tool switches; fixed 20-step TTL tool-schema pinning achieves $21.80 \pm 0.49\%$ hit rate ($142.08\%$ clean Stall Ratio, and $19.15 \pm 0.46\%$ on the 32-tool Gorilla trace due to HBM pinning exhaustion); a horizon-matched $\text{TTL} = 8$-step tool-pinning policy (matching ELLA's $\approx 7.70$-step idle-tool crossover) improves to $24.12 \pm 0.45\%$ hit rate ($136.04\%$ clean Stall Ratio, and $22.40 \pm 0.44\%$ on 32-tool Gorilla); and an HBM-utilization-throttled adaptive TTL policy (dynamically shortening tool TTL from $8$ to $3$ steps whenever HBM occupancy exceeds $85\%$) reaches $24.85 \pm 0.43\%$ hit rate ($134.72\%$ clean Stall Ratio, and $24.10 \pm 0.42\%$ on 32-tool Gorilla). ELLA's continuous exponential decay ($\lambda_{\text{tool}}=0.08$), forward DAG lookahead, and branch entropy $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$ outperform even adaptive TTL by $+3.46$ percentage points in hit rate ($28.31\%$ vs.\ $24.85\%$, and $+5.35$ percentage points on 32-tool Gorilla: $29.45\%$ vs.\ $24.10\%$): comparing \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} ($28.31\%$ hit rate, $127.34\%$ Stall Ratio, $98.64\%$ QK attention) against \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA} ($\mathcal{H}_i(t)\equiv 0$, KVFlow-style static DAG prefetching~\cite{pan2025kvflow}, $26.41\%$ hit rate, $131.08\%$ Stall Ratio, $96.45\%$ QK attention) isolates the exact contribution of $\mathcal{H}_i(t)$: $+1.90$ percentage points ($+7.19\%$ relative) in hit rate, $+2.19$ percentage points in QK attention retention, and a $3.74$ percentage point ($2.85\%$ relative) reduction in Stall Ratio.

\subsection{Multi-Turn Workload Generalization and Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
Table~\ref{tab:generalization_sensitivity} evaluates ELLA's generalization across parameterized multi-turn workload emulations, multi-tenant heterogeneity, and hyperparameter perturbations at $K=20$: (1) on \textbf{SWE-bench-calibrated repository debugging emulation traces}~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} (parameterized to 12 turns/stream where agents revisit prior-turn reasoning scratchpad blocks with $25\%$ probability during patch verification and test-failure backtracking, allocating $64$ private physical pages/stream [$4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}, 16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, 4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{priv}}, 40\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}}$] plus the 16-page shared prefix $= 1{,}296$ allocated physical pages inside $N_{\text{host}}=2{,}500$, with online 2-step lookahead driven by the mode-selected router prediction $\arg\max_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{act}}} \tilde{p}_{i,m}(v)$ under a realistic $p_{\text{err}} \approx 12.8\%$ branch misprediction rate rather than a future oracle), \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} sustains a \textbf{26.82 $\pm$ 0.42\% hit rate} (outperforming \texttt{DEMAND\_LRU} at $0.45\%$, \texttt{DEMAND\_LFU} at $2.10\%$, \texttt{SGLang} at $15.10\%$, \texttt{CachedAttention} at $21.40\%$, \texttt{InfiniGen} at $22.65\%$, and \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA} at $24.50\%$) and \textbf{96.42 $\pm$ 0.24\%} empirical QK attention retention; (2) on \textbf{Gorilla-calibrated multi-turn API catalog emulation traces}~\cite{patil2024gorilla} (extending Gorilla's OpenAPI tool catalogs to stateful BFCL-style 10-turn tool-switching workflows across $32$ OpenAPI tool-schema pages per stream—comprising $16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{shared}}$ in a 20-page shared prefix plus $16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{tool}}^{\text{priv}}$ per stream alongside $4\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{active}}, 16\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{hist}}, 24\ \mathcal{P}_{\text{scratch}} = 60$ private pages/stream, totaling $1{,}220$ allocated physical pages inside $N_{\text{host}}=2{,}500$ under mode-selected router lookahead and relative priority-margin headroom gating), rapid idle-tool decay ($\lambda_{\text{tool}}=0.08$) allows newly predicted tool schemas and DAG-boosted history to preempt dormant schemas within $\approx 8$ steps, achieving a \textbf{29.45 $\pm$ 0.39\% hit rate} (vs.\ $17.25\%$ for \texttt{SGLang}, $24.80\%$ for \texttt{InfiniGen}, and $27.30\%$ for \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA}); (3) on a \textbf{Heterogeneous 20-Prompt Multi-Tenant Workload} ($K=20$ independent streams with distinct per-stream hot working-set sizes spanning $3.0\text{--}7.5$ pages/stream and heterogeneous turn lengths spanning $6\text{--}14$ steps/turn across $2{,}040$ accesses), while LFU's capacity cliff softens from $1.25\%$ to $8.45 \pm 0.52\%$ as smaller streams retain partial residency, \texttt{SPECULATIVE\_ELLA} achieves $\mathbf{28.25 \pm 0.44\%}$ hit rate ($\mathbf{127.42 \pm 0.76\%}$ Stall Ratio), outperforming LFU by $+19.80$ percentage points and \texttt{STATIC\_DAG\_ELLA} ($26.35\%$) by $+1.90$ percentage points; and (4) across $\pm 50\%$ decay-rate perturbations to $(\lambda, \lambda_{\text{tool}}, \mu)$ and $B_{\text{DAG}} \in \{250, 500\}$ ($-29\% / +43\%$, lower-bounded at $250$ so DAG-boosted history retains strictly higher priority than dormant tool-schema blocks at $C_{\text{floor}} = 200$), 5-seed mean hit rate varies by $< 1.2$ percentage points ($27.42\%\text{--}28.62\%$) and Stall Ratio varies by $< 2.3$ percentage points ($126.75\%\text{--}129.02\%$), confirming high robustness without brittle tuning.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented Speculative Epistemic KV-Cache Paging (SE-Paging) and the Epistemic Least-Likely-Attended (ELLA) eviction protocol for multi-turn agentic LLM serving. By coupling agent workflow DAG lookahead and conditionally re-normalized action-delimiter epistemic branch entropy to physical memory management, SE-Paging protects attention sinks, tool schemas, and floored history while overlapping DMA transfers with matrix compute in our calibrated $1:32$ physical tensor-buffer emulator and multi-turn trace replays. Future work includes integrating SE-Paging directly with on-device C++/Pallas TPU v6e hardware DMA descriptor rings on full-scale distributed SWE-bench~\cite{jimenez2024swebench} and Gorilla~\cite{patil2024gorilla} production clusters.
